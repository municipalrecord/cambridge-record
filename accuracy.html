How accurate is this site?

2026, read against the Clerk’s record

On 4 October 2026 we read every 2026 record, not a sample: 1,206 in all (663 items on a council night, 44 committee meetings and 499 document pages). Each was read from the city’s own documents and checked against our pages; 121 were read twice by different readers. The first reading found 29 mistakes, and a second reading found 1 more. We fixed each one in how the site is built, never by hand in a name, date, tally or outcome, and read every changed record again until a reading found none. On the pages published now, every 2026 record has been read, and none is wrong or missing.

773 records match exactly. 433 differ in ways we judged would mislead no one. We list them so you can judge for yourself:

  • The clerk names who made a motion; our page names no one. (333)
  • A contact detail of a city office or an organisation is blanked, by mistake on our side. (27)
  • A committee vote kept off the committee page, by design. (20)
  • A voice vote on the council's own business, such as suspending its rules, is not listed. (11)
  • Steps of an item listed out of order on the night. (6)
  • Scanned pages shown blank, with no note saying why. (6)
  • A cut-off clerk's stamp or title fragment on a meeting card. (5)
  • A typing slip in a heading, usually the city's own. (3)
  • Other kinds, one to three records each, each listed in the audit file. (22)

Before the full reading, we checked 2026 by random samples:

On 3 October 2026 we drew a random sample of 130 records from 2026 pages with a published seed. Each was read from the city’s own documents, then checked against our pages. 6 of 130 had a mistake (4.6%, 95% interval 1.7% to 9.8%).

  • A page said the council's minutes did not record an item that they did record, naming it by its place on the agenda. Six pages carried the line.
  • A meeting card showed the Final Actions sheet's two-step version of a vote where the minutes record one roll call.
  • A committee page left out the two presentations its agenda lists.
  • Two document pages and their downloads showed residents' personal contact details. Both are now withheld.
  • An older item's page offered another item's 2026 documents for download.

After fixes, a second sample on 4 October 2026 found 3 of 130 (2.3%, 95% interval 0.5% to 6.6%). It is the sixth sample drawn after fixes, and the latest; every one is in the audit file. Every miss is listed, with the city’s page and ours.

Votes & outcomes audit

Roll calls, outcomes, and sponsors on item pages come from a crawl of the city's own item records. Same discipline: we drew 150 crawled items at random (seed 20260713), fetched each city page fresh, re-parsed it, and compared status, sponsors, and every named vote. Result: 150/150 exact · 0 divergent · 0 fetch errors. raw results · audit code. Separately, every one of the 6,099 recorded roll calls must satisfy the city's own tally (“[4 TO 5]” must equal exactly 4 + 5 parsed names) or it is withheld from display. 0 failed that check in the current build.

Method, honestly

The comparison is against what the city's portal displays today: if the clerk edits a record after we capture it, that surfaces here as a deviation. The auditor is software too, so the code and raw results above exist for checking the checker. This page regenerates with every audit run. Found something wrong anyway? Every page has an error-report link in its footer, and corrections are public.
