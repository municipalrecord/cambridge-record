How accurate is this site?

2026, read against the Clerk’s record

On 4 October 2026 we read every 2026 record, not a sample: 1,206 in all (663 items on a council night, 44 committee meetings and 499 document pages). Each was read from the city’s own documents and checked against our pages; 121 were read twice by different readers. The first reading found 29 mistakes, and a second reading found 1 more. We fixed each one in how the site is built, never by hand in a name, date, tally or outcome, and read every changed record again until a reading found none. On the pages published now, every 2026 record has been read, and none is wrong or missing.

773 records match exactly. 433 differ in ways we judged would mislead no one. We list them so you can judge for yourself:

  • The clerk names who made a motion; our page names no one. (333)
  • A contact detail of a city office or an organisation is blanked, by mistake on our side. (27)
  • A committee vote kept off the committee page, by design. (20)
  • A voice vote on the council's own business, such as suspending its rules, is not listed. (11)
  • Steps of an item listed out of order on the night. (6)
  • Scanned pages shown blank, with no note saying why. (6)
  • A cut-off clerk's stamp or title fragment on a meeting card. (5)
  • A typing slip in a heading, usually the city's own. (3)
  • Other kinds, one to three records each, each listed in the audit file. (22)

Checking the check (4 and 5 October 2026)

A full reading can still be wrong in ways it cannot see: the readers and the checks share one method. So we tested that method four ways, and we publish what each found, misses included.

  • Planted errors. We put 31 deliberate errors into a copy of the data and the pages, drawn by rule with seed 2610050: swapped ayes and nays, flipped results, wrong dates, votes moved to the wrong item, wrong files behind pages, changed movers and sponsors, a withheld document released. Then we ran every check again, blind. They caught 26 of 31. The 5 they missed:

    • A stored vote's tally changed by one. The page kept the right count and added a line that the city's portal differs; the grader called that harmless. A new check now reads every tally against its names.
    • A co-sponsor vote deleted. The same vote is stored twice, so the page still showed it, two steps in a different order.
    • A hand-written note removed from a meeting card, which then showed one of the night's four results. Graded harmless; the first reading had counted the same fault a miss.
    • A committee vote's tally changed by one. It shows only in the data explorer's table, which no reading covers. The new tally check reads it.
    • A withheld document released to the public. Nothing caught it: no reading covers a page that did not exist before, and the privacy gate passed. A new check now stops any build that releases a withheld document.

    3 of the 5 are now caught by two checks added that day and run on every build. The other two are plain-sight gaps only a page-by-page reading catches. The full table.

  • A second sample, new readers. On 4 October 2026 we drew 100 more records (seed 2610052) and had two new readers read each one from the city's documents, without seeing the first reading; the graders had not seen its grades. 0 of 100 were wrong (95% interval 0% to 3.6%). Counted strictly, 2 of 100 (0.2% to 7.0%): two scanned pages shown blank with no notice, a kind the full reading had called harmless and which every such page now says in a sentence. The two readers agreed on 97 of 100. The result.

  • Against the local paper. Of the 2026 council votes Cambridge Day reported, we checked 12 against our pages, chosen by rule: every split roll call first, then ordinances, then failures. 9 agree on every fact the story states, 2 agree in part, 1 story reports a briefing and no vote, and none contradicts the record. One count differs: the vote to end debate on POR 2026-115 on 1 June: the paper says it passed 6 to 3; the minutes, page 21, print 6 yes, 2 no and 1 present, and our page shows the minutes' count. The twelve.

  • Every page says what it leaves out. On 5 October 2026 we read every 2026 page for what it does not show, and gave each such page one sentence saying so: 386 sentences in all. Among them:

    • 4 council meetings read from the verbatim transcript, because the minutes are not out
    • 29 committee meetings whose 102 votes are in the minutes and not on the page
    • 75 scanned pages shown blank on 11 document pages
    • 3 items with a document withheld for residents' personal details
    • 247 items where the clerk's minutes may name a mover the page does not show
    • 17 bodies with meetings on the city's portal that are not on this site yet, and the council's own meeting of 28 September

    A check that every such page carries its sentence runs on every build. The inventory.

  • No person has read the record yet. Every reading and every grading in the four tests above was done by AI models, not by a person. A later check, kept apart from these, puts a person in front of the city's own pages: twenty 2026 votes, ten drawn at random and ten chosen by rule as the hardest kinds (the closest splits, failures, votes with a member away, votes the site keeps off its pages), read by someone who has not seen our pages. That read has not been done. When it is, its result goes here, misses included.

How accurate are the 2020, 2021 and 2022 pages?

Checked 12 to 27 October 2026. Final build f41. The full working files are AUDIT_2020_2022.md, VALIDATE_2020_2022.md and the validate/ folder beside them.

Every page on this site for the council's work in 2020, 2021 and 2022 was read against the city's own records, twice, by readers who had not seen the pages before. Every fault found was fixed, and every page that changed was read again, until one build had no fault left. Then the finished pages were tested four more ways. This section says what was checked, what was found, what was fixed, and what has not been checked.

What was checked

A census is a list of everything on the pages that a reader could check against the city's record: each meeting night, each item, each committee meeting, each document. Each entry is a row. A row is "exact" when the page matches the record, "benign" when it differs in a way that misleads no one (a vote filed under the night's council business rather than the item, for instance), "wrong" when the page says something the record does not, and "missing" when the record holds something the page leaves out.

What no person has read

No person has read a 2020-2022 row for this check. Every read above was by a machine reader against the city's record, graded by another. The person's read is its own stage after this one, by Dan's ruling of 9 October 2026, and this section will say when it has happened.

Also unread, by design: every document whose text is held. Also not checked: the School Committee and the boards that are not council committees; the "request" reading of orders that ask the administration to act, which rests on one read of the portal's order texts; and the four 2022 board appointments whose wording waits on a legal check of the charter in force that year.
