# The disposition gap: how much of the 71% is actually missing

An audit of the 29,528 published agenda items (of 41,455) that render:

> "The clerk's record shows no vote or disposition against this item..."

All numbers below are **exact counts** over the full corpus unless marked
otherwise. Generated by `audit/disposition_gap.py`; raw figures in
`audit/corpus/disposition_gap_stats.json`.

---

## Numbers first

| | count | share of 29,528 |
|---|---|---|
| Items showing "no vote or disposition" | **29,528** | 100% |
| **Recoverable right now from data the site already ships** | **3** | 0.01% |
| Recoverable from minutes the site itself records as "on file" | 23,838 | 80.7% |
| Genuinely absent — minutes not on file | 288 | 1.0% |
| Untestable — meeting missing from the site's own meetings table | 5,399 | 18.3% |

**The single number that matters: 3.** Not 3,000. Three.

The headline is not that the site failed to link outcomes it holds. It is
that **the site's outcome datasets do not exist before 2016 at all**, and
28,719 of the 29,528 (97.3%) are pre-2016 items.

---

## 1. Recoverable now from votes-rows / items-rows: 3 items

Joined `items.jsonl` to `votes-rows` (23,415 rows) and `items-rows`
(11,754 rows) on a canonical key derived from the row's own href
(`../items/app-2026-39.html` → `app-2026-39`), falling back to a
normalised label parse that handles every observed ID form:
`APP 2026 #39`, `RES 2016 # 16`, `ORD 2016 # 3`, `AR-16-1` (two-digit
year), `POR 2014-4`. Only 10 of 23,415 vote rows and 1 of 11,754 item
rows failed to yield a key.

**Result:**

| finding | count |
|---|---|
| No-disposition items appearing *at all* in votes-rows | 2 |
| No-disposition items appearing *at all* in items-rows | 67 |
| ...of those 67, rows carrying a non-empty Outcome | **0** |
| No-disposition items with a committee vote attached | 1 |
| **Total recoverable** | **3** |

The three are written to `audit/corpus/recoverable_dispositions.jsonl`:

- **COF 2022-92** — votes-rows ships a full named roll call, `8-0-1`,
  eight councillors listed voting yes and Nolan absent, dated 2022-08-01.
  The item page nonetheless renders `<p class='noact'>`. Confidence: high.
- **COF 2023-56** — same shape: `9-0-0`, nine named yes votes, 2023-04-03.
  Confidence: high.
- **COF 2024-57** — `Motion passed [11 TO 0]`, Charter Review Committee,
  2024-06-05, from committee-votes-rows. Confidence: medium (a committee
  motion is not a Council disposition).

The first two are a real, if tiny, rendering bug with a clear cause: the
clerk's sheet gave a tally with **no outcome verb**, so the generator's
"is there a disposition" test saw no verb and fell through to the
no-disposition text — while discarding a recorded roll call with named
voters. 4,536 vote rows corpus-wide are tally-only; only these 2 land on
items that show no disposition, but the bug class is real.

### Why the number is so small — the join is sound, the data is absent

Two independent checks confirm this is not a broken join.

**Control group.** Of the 11,927 items that *do* display a disposition,
11,863 (99.5%) match a shipped outcome row. Only 64 do not, and those are
almost all AR (awaiting-report) items whose disposition is "answered by
CMA 2016 #37" rather than a vote. The site renders a disposition
essentially *iff* a shipped row has one — so the join reproduces the
site's own logic.

**Hand checks.** Verified by hand: `por-2014-4` (dash form, matches
`POR 2014 #4`, page shows "Order adopted"), `app-2026-39` (hash form),
`res-2016-16` (space-hash form), `ar-2016-1` (two-digit-year dash form
`AR-16-1`), and all three recovered items against their rendered HTML
(`grep "class='noact'"` confirms each still shows the no-disposition text).

**Coverage by year.** Distinct item keys present in the outcome datasets:

| year | corpus items | no-disposition | keys in votes-rows | keys in items-rows |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2004–2013 | 24,909 | 24,909 (100%) | **0** | **0** |
| 2014 | 1,803 | 1,802 | 1 | 1 |
| 2015 | 2,015 | 2,008 | 7 | 7 |
| 2016 | 1,350 | 33 | 1,316 | 1,306 |
| 2017–2025 | 10,784 | 663 | ~9,900 | ~9,800 |
| 2026 | 594 | 113 | 481 | 16 |

There is a **hard cliff at 2016**. Before it, the site ships no outcome
data whatsoever; after it, coverage is near-total. The 71% figure is
almost entirely an artefact of that cliff.

The 4,252 vote keys that match no corpus item are overwhelmingly `COM`
communications (4,126) plus `CC`/`CRT`/`REC` types that have no item
pages — expected, not a join failure.

### By era — the dominant split

|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2004–2015 | 28,727 | 28,719 | **100.0%** | 97.3% |
| 2016–2026 | 12,728 | 809 | 6.4% | 2.7% |

### By type (whole corpus — dominated by the era effect)

| type | no-disp | total | rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| ARS | 62 | 62 | 100.0% |
| RES | 16,788 | 20,549 | 81.7% |
| COF | 916 | 1,188 | 77.1% |
| ORD | 177 | 253 | 70.0% |
| POR | 6,093 | 8,950 | 68.1% |
| APP | 1,242 | 1,902 | 65.3% |
| CMA | 4,246 | 7,520 | 56.5% |
| AR | 0 | 1,027 | 0.0% |

The ceremonial-resolution hypothesis does **not** explain the gap. RES is
81.7% but POR (policy orders — substantive, always voted), ORD
(ordinations) and APP (curb cuts, licences — each requires a Council
grant) sit at 68–70% and 65%. A curb-cut application with no recorded
disposition is not "received and filed"; something was granted or denied.
AR at 0.0% is the tell: AR items are the one type whose disposition is
derived from a linked CMA answer rather than a vote sheet, and they are
complete across all years — so completeness tracks the *derivation
method*, not the item's ceremonial status.

### Anomalies within the modern era (2016+), where outcome data exists

Whole-corpus anomaly scoring is meaningless (every pre-2016 cell is 100%).
Scoring each type against its own 2016–2026 median, cells with ≥20 items

| COF | 2026 | 48.3% | 75.6% | −27.3 | 43 |
| COF | 2025 | 57.9% | 75.6% | −17.7 | 33 |

Modern no-disposition items by type: **COF 647, ARS 62, CMA 41, POR 28,
RES 16, APP 9, ORD 2, HR 2, HS 2** (809 total).

**The suspicious cells, in priority order:**

1. **CMA 2026 (38 items, 16.5%) and POR 2026 (14 items, 10.5%).** Every
   other year for these types is 0.0%. City Manager agenda items and
   policy orders are always acted on. This is the 2026 backlog: the clerk
   has published no minutes after 2026-05-04, so the outcome sheets are
   simply not out yet. Correct, but the page text ("the clerk's record
   shows no vote") reads as an assertion about the record rather than a
   pending-publication notice.
2. **ARS 2024–2026: 62 of 62, 100%.** These are the only items that
   appear in items-rows *with an empty Outcome cell* — the site has a row
   for each and the row says nothing. Worth checking whether ARS items
   have dispositions at all as a class.
3. **COF, 75.6% median across 11 modern years.** The largest modern block
   (647 items) and the most stable. Communications from City Officers
   plausibly are placed on file without a vote, but the swing from 94.0%
   (2020) to 31.4% (2023) with no change in item type suggests the rate
   tracks how a given year's sheets were transcribed, not what the
   Council did.

## 3. Minutes on file vs not on file

Joined each no-disposition item to `meetings-rows` on (item date, body),
mapping "Cambridge City Council" → "City Council". No City Council date
carries both an on-file and a not-on-file row, so classification is
unambiguous.

| | count | share |
|---|---|---|
| On a meeting whose **minutes are on file** | **23,840** | 80.7% |
| On a meeting whose minutes are **not on file** | 288 | 1.0% |
| Meeting absent from the meetings table entirely | 5,380 | 18.2% |
| Item carries no date at all | 20 | 0.1% |

**This is the finding that settles the question.** Four in five
no-disposition items sit on meetings the site itself records as having
minutes on file. "The city never published it" accounts for **1.0%**.

Spot-checked by hand: APP 2004-1 (2004-01-12) → "Regular City Council
Meeting (Sullivan Chamber) MEETING HELD." minutes **on file**;
RES 2005-100, CMA 2011-200 likewise; COF 2022-92 → Special Meeting,
138 items, on file. POR 2013-1 → no row (2013 hole, below).

### What "Why not" says

Only two distinct values across all 288:

- **234 blank.** No reason recorded at all — the overwhelming majority.
  The column is empty for every pre-2016 not-on-file meeting.
- **54:** "the clerk has published no minutes for any meeting after
  May 4, 2026; checked against the city's portal on Sep 8, 2026" — the
  2026 publication backlog, the only case where the site actually
  explains the absence.

Corpus-wide the meetings table has 1,097 not-on-file rows: 1,086 blank,
11 carrying the 2026 backlog note. **The "Why not" column is ~99% empty**,
which is itself an accountability gap — the site asserts minutes are
missing without saying how it knows.

### The meetings table has year-sized holes

The 5,380 unmatched items are not scattered; 5,233 of them sit on dates
absent from the meetings table under *any* body:

| year | unmatched no-disp items | City Council rows in meetings-rows |
|---|---|---|
| 2010 | 440 | 59 |
| 2012 | 1,097 | 15 |
| **2013** | **1,882 (all of 2013)** | **0** |
| 2014 | 1,802 (all but 1) | 1 |
| 2019–2026 | 159 | 36–44/yr |

2013 is missing from the meetings table completely; 2014 has one row;
2012 has 15. Every other year 2003–2011 has 59–122. Those three years
cannot be audited against minutes at all until the meetings table is
backfilled. Separately, the site publishes council meeting *pages* only
for 2016+ (plus one 2014) — 391 pages against 1,381 City Council meeting
rows.

## 4. Internal inconsistency: cite_disposition vs the page — zero

**0 items** show the no-disposition text while carrying a disposition
parsed from their own citation string. 2,842 items have a non-empty
`cite_disposition`, and every one of them renders a disposition. Clean —
no internal contradiction in the site's own output on this axis.

(The near-miss is the tally-only case in §1: the source data has the

### By type

| type | no citation | total | rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| AR | 1,027 | 1,027 | **100.0%** |
| POR | 3,247 | 8,950 | 36.3% |
| CMA | 2,602 | 7,520 | 34.6% |
| COF | 411 | 1,188 | 34.6% |
| RES | 5,600 | 20,549 | 27.3% |
| ORD | 39 | 253 | 15.4% |
| APP | 182 | 1,902 | 9.6% |
| ARS / HR / HS | 0 | 66 | 0.0% |

### By year

**Zero before 2014.** Then: 2014 100%, 2015 99.9%, 2016 76.3%,
2017 78.9%, 2018 76.5%, 2019 74.6%, 2020 78.5%, 2021 80.9%, 2022 74.2%,
2023 69.8%, 2024 72.5%, 2025 65.6%, 2026 30.8%.

### Correlation with the no-disposition set: strongly NEGATIVE

|  | no disposition | has disposition |
|---|---|---|
| **no citation** | 4,125 | 8,983 |
| **has citation** | 25,403 | 2,944 |

φ = **−0.597**. These are close to opposite populations. 89.7% of items
*with* a citation show no disposition; only 31.5% of items *without* one
do. All 13,103 no-citation items with a date got it from a meeting link,
not a citation string — i.e. `has_citation=false` marks items whose
citation string could not be parsed, which is a **2014-onward** failure,
whereas the no-disposition gap is a **pre-2016** failure. They are two
different defects with an inverse relationship, not one problem.

Only 2 of the 3 recoverable items are in the no-citation set.

## Recoverable now

**Tier A — fix today, from bytes already on the server: 3 items.**
Written to `audit/corpus/recoverable_dispositions.jsonl`. Two are a
generator bug (tally-only outcome with named voters discarded because the
string has no verb); one is a committee motion never surfaced on the item
page. *Method:* treat a tally-shaped Outcome with a non-empty "Voted yes"
or "Voted no" as a disposition; surface committee-votes-rows on item
pages.

**Tier B — recoverable from the city's record, which the site already
says it holds: 23,838 items** (23,271 legacy, 567 modern). Every one sits
on a meeting whose minutes the site's own meetings table marks "on file".
The disposition exists in a published document; nobody has extracted it.
*Method:* fetch and parse the on-file minutes for the 619 City Council
meeting dates marked on file, chiefly 2004–2011 and 2015. This requires
network work, not a rendering change — it is the actual audit, and it is
where ~81% of the gap lives. **This figure is an upper bound on what
minutes could yield**: it counts items on on-file meetings, not items
verified to have a disposition inside those minutes.

**Tier C — genuinely absent: 288 items** (1.0%). Minutes not on file;
54 of them are the 2026 backlog, which will resolve when the clerk
publishes.

**Tier D — untestable until the site fixes its own metadata: 5,399
items** (18.3%). 5,233 sit on meeting dates missing from meetings-rows
entirely — all of 2013, nearly all of 2014, most of 2012, part of 2010.
20 items carry no date at all. *Method:* backfill the meetings table for
2010–2014 first; that alone moves ~5,200 items into a testable tier.

### Bottom line

The site's invitation — "if the city's record shows otherwise, that is
exactly what an audit should catch" — is answered as follows. The city's
record almost certainly *does* show otherwise for ~23,800 items, because
the site's own data says the minutes are on file. But that is a claim
about documents the site has not parsed, not about data it is sitting on
and failing to display. On the narrow question asked — what can be fixed
from shipped data — the answer is **3 items**, plus 5,233 that a
meetings-table backfill would make auditable.

The honest revision to the disposition sentence would distinguish three
cases it currently collapses into one: *no outcome was recorded*, *we have
not yet read the minutes that would record it*, and *the clerk has not
published yet*. Today 97.3% of the items showing that sentence are the
second case.
