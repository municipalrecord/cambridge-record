What is ShotSpotter:  ShotSpotter is a network of acoustic sensors that can detect, locate, and alert police to gunshot incidents.  Installation of the system was approved by the Cambridge City Council in 2014.  All costs associated with the system for Cambridge and several other cities are paid for as part of an Urban Area Security Initiative (UASI) grant program.  SoundThinking, Inc., the owner of ShotSpotter, installed the sensors and continues to maintain them.  Locations of the sensors is not publicly disclosed to protect the integrity of the system. 9-1-1 Call vs. ShotSpotter:  ShotSpotter is an automated technology that is activated by potential gunshot incidents and verified in real time by trained professionals. This results in prompt notifications to law enforcement, which can facilitate a faster response.  Traditional 911 calls are dependent on people taking action. If a person does not have immediate access to a phone, know their location or chooses not to call, it adversely impacts the emergency response.  From 2015 – Present there have been 11 confirmed shootings that were picked up by ShotSpotter but did not result in any 9-1-1 calls. How Cambridge Responds:  The initial number of officers dispatched to a 9-1-1 call reporting gunshots and a ShotSpotter activation is the same.  With a ShotSpotter activation, Officers working will receive the alert directly on their phone and cruiser computer.  When Emergency Communications (ECC) receives an alert and will immediately dispatch nearby officers to investigate.  When officers arrive they look for any active threat, victims, ballistic evidence or potential witnesses.  If it is confirmed that a shooting occurred additional resources will respond to assist victims, process the crime scene and speak with witnesses. If there is no evidence of a shooting, officers will clear the area and return to normal patrol duties. Unfounded Alerts: An unfounded alert does not definitively mean a shooting did not occur. ◦More simply put, an unfounded alert means no evidence was located to prove there had been a shooting. ◦No victims ◦No ballistic evidence ◦No witnesses ◦Occasionally alerts are proven to be false, mainly caused due to the following: ◦Loud industrial work or construction noises ◦Fireworks ◦A car backfiring or loudly crashing ◦Electrical transformer explosion. Common Misconceptions: Does ShotSpotter record private conversations? - No The system is only activated when 3 or more sensors are triggered by an explosive sound measuring 120 decibels. For comparison, an average human voice is 60 decibels and will not trigger any sensors. There has never been a conversation recorded in Cambridge Does ShotSpotter result in over-policing? - No Massachusetts Courts have said a ShotSpotter alert alone does not give a police officer reasonable suspicion to stop someone. There have never been any use of force incidents or community complaints resulting from a CPD response to a ShotSpotter alert. Is ShotSpotter Utilized for Immigration Enforcement? - No Data captured by ShotSpotter (explosive sounds registering 120 decibels) would not have any use to immigration enforcement. There have been no incidents where Immigration & Customs Enforcement (ICE) has requested any ShotSpotter data from CPD or SoundThinking. Release of any such data would require a judicial subpoena and be limited to a specific criminal investigation of a gunshot incident.
