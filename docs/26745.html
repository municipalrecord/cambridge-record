DISCUSSION The Open Meeting Law was enacted "to eliminate much of the secrecy surrounding deliberation and decisions on which public policy is based." Ghiglione v. School Committee of Southbridge, 376 Mass. 70, 72 (1978). Except in an emergency, "a public body shall post notice of every meeting at least 48 hours prior to such meeting, excluding Saturdays, Sundays and legal holidays." G.L. c. 30A, § 20(b). A "meeting" is defined, in relevant part, as "a deliberation by a public body with respect to any matter within the body's jurisdiction." G.L. c. 30A, § 18. The law defines "deliberation" as "an oral or written communication through any medium, including electronic mail, between or among a quorum of a public body on any public business within its jurisdiction..." Id. For the purposes of the Open Meeting Law, a "quorum" is a simple majority of the members of a public body. Id.; see also G.L. c. 43 § 99. A public body may not engage in serial communication whereby a quorum communicates in a non-contemporaneous manner outside of a meeting on a subject within the public body's jurisdiction. See McCrea v. Flaherty, 71 Mass. App. Ct. 637, 648-49 (2008). The Council did not violate the Open Meeting Law because the Policy Order was originally introduced by less than a quorum of the members of the City Council, then the Policy Order was publicly discussed at the June 2n° meeting, and thereafter the alleged discussion about an amendment to the Policy Order on or prior to June 9, 2025, occurred between two Council members, which is not a quorum of the Council. The Council is a nine-member public body; thus, five members constitute a quorum. See Cambridge Plan E Charter, G.L. c. 43 $§ 96, 99 (2022), attached hereto as Exhibit B. There is no allegation or evidence that the purported discussion about the amendment involved more than two Council members, that either Vice Mayor McGovern or Councilor Siddiqui had any serial communications, nor that any additional communications about the Policy Order amendment occurred outside the public Council meetings. Accordingly, because the discussion identified in Teague's Complaint did not involve a quorum, it did not violate the Open Meeting Law. Additionally, Vice Mayor McGovern and Councilor Siddiqui's discussion about the amendment is separate from any discussion about the original Policy Order, which was discussed at a public meeting, and therefore there was no serial communication. CONCLUSION As stated above, there was no violation of the Open Meeting Law by the City Council, and therefore no further action is necessary in this regard.
