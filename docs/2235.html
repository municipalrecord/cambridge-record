A communication was received from Paula M. Crane, Deputy City Clerk, transmitting a report from Councillor Nadeem Mazen, Chair of the Neighborhood and Long Term Planning, Public Facilities, Arts and Celebration Committee, for a public hearing held on June 23, 2016 to discuss the formation of a special working group that will be tasked with developing a framework for the continued stewardship, curatorship and oversight of the Out of Town News Kiosk in Harvard Square.

Attachment R CAMBRIDGE Paula Crane To: From: Robyn Culbertson [email removed] Date: June 27, 2016 Re: Redesign of Out-of-Town News Kiosk Paula, First, I would like to thank you for including the Cambridge Office for Tourism in the process for the redesign for the Out-of-Town News Kiosk and its subsequent stewardship. It is important that this remains an inclusive process on all levels, and I was happy to attend the meeting on June 23rd One of the major concerns that our office has had since these discussions have begun is that the Information Booth next to the Out-of-Town News kiosk is currently the only "welcome center" for the City of Cambridge. The size of that booth limits the amount of materials and personnel that can be accommodated there, and thus we are limited to the information and services we can provide. It is our sincere desire to have the news kiosk offer space for us to better serve the entire community and its visitors. For that reason, we would recommend that the programming of the repurposed news kiosk be overseen by an advisory board, consisting of representatives from all of the vested partners including but not limited to the City of Cambridge, the Cambridge Office for Tourism, Harvard University and the Harvard Square Business Association. It is our feeling that the Harvard Square Business Association as done an outstanding job of initiating discussions and exploring the possibilities for the kiosk. That said, the kiosk itself, while physically located in Harvard Square, is positioned to serve a much broader audience. Harvard Square is often the first introduction visitors have to Cambridge, but it is always our office's goal to expand the visitors' experience and to show them what other the "squares" and neighborhoods have to offer. If the Visitor Information Booth is to be removed to make way for the redesign, then it is our goal to ensure that the new facility will provide services that welcome visitors to ALL of Cambridge. Thank you for your consideration, and I look forward to continued discussions regarding this exciting project.
