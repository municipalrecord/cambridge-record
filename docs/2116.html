Addressing Construction Street Hazards in Cambridge Through an ordinance requiring road hazards be brightly painted yellow and/or reducing the angle of obstruction to 30 degrees above the horizontal plane
Page 2 of 11
• 12.12.065 – Maintenance of safe passage—Proper marking of hazards and trench closure required. • A. Whenever any street is opened, occupied, obstructed or encumbered by any department of the City, or any other party to include contractors and subcontractors, workers much ensure that hazards in the work area must be clearly marked and safely managed to mitigate dangers to cyclists by: • 1. Mark all vertical surfaces of 1 inch or more that are more than 30 degrees off of horizontal with bright yellow paint on the entire vertical surface(s) and at least ½ of the horizontal surface; • B. At all seam areas, where the roadwork is milled up to existing surface, pavement will be either ground at no greater than a 30 degree angle or have a ramp of asphalt fill ensuring that the contact point between the milled work and the existing surface has a ramp of no more than 30 degrees and the entire length of the seam area will be marked with bright yellow paint; • 1. Ensuring that the paint marking hazards is refreshed as needed to maintain the required coverage; • 2. Ensuring that all open trench or patching work will be closed by the end of business on Fridays except when weekend work is scheduled. • C. Violations of the paint requirement would be viewed as clear evidence of negligence on the part of whatever party was doing the street work.
Page 3 of 11
The Challenge Roadwork frequently results in exposed steel castings, shifts in pavement height and other hazards to cyclists during construction that can and do lead to accidents and injuries as cyclists, distracted by traffic, jaywalkers and/or weather, fail to see these street hazards in time to avoid them. This issue is compounded that the paint used to mark, albeit inconsistently, some of these hazards is not always visible to people with color vision challenges.
