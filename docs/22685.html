What is CPP? Cambridge Preschool Program 4
Page 5 of 29
CPP is an ambitious, newly-launched program that seeks to expand access to high- quality preschool programming for Cambridge families. The program has been designed to ensure that all young children in Cambridge, and especially those furthest from access to resources and opportunity, are set up for success in kindergarten and beyond. What are the goals of CPP? • Expand preschool access – To ensure that all Cambridge four- year-olds (and some three-year-olds) have access to high-quality early childhood education. Through the system design, children from low-income families are prioritized for preschool seats. • Enhance preschool quality – To ensure that Cambridge preschool programs provide high-quality, evidence-based early childhood programming to children and families. • Connect and align the early childhood ecosystem – To support a high-quality and more aligned B-Grade 3 continuum. 5
Page 6 of 29
What is a mixed-delivery system? • An approach to providing Universal Pre-Kindergarten (UPK) that distributes Pre-K seats across a variety of service- delivery settings. In systems across the country, preschool settings can include public schools, charter schools, community-based preschool / childcare programs, Head Start programs, etc. • In Cambridge, the mixed-delivery system includes CPS classrooms, DHSP classrooms, and community- based programs. What are the requirements for programs to be included in CPP? In order to be a CPP provider, preschool programs must: • Provide full-day, school-year preschool (at least a 6.5-hour day). • Provide high-quality preschool programming. • Starting in 2015, a number of community providers participated in the Cambridge Scholarship Program, the precursor to CPP. • Early 2020 – All Cambridge preschool programs were invited to an information session, and subsequently invited to indicate interest in becoming a CPP partner. • All interested programs participated in quality / needs assessment. Those that met the requirements above were included in CPP. How were CPP programs selected? • Research-based, state-approved curriculum • Highly-qualified educators • Focus on social-emotional development and school readiness through play-based learning CPP is a publicly-funded program that provides free, school-day, school-year preschool within a mixed-delivery system, to all four-year-olds (and some three- year-olds) who are Cambridge residents. 6

The CPP mixed-delivery system includes a wide range of preschool program options. • CPP includes 23 community-based programs across 42 sites. (Some programs have multiple sites.) • Of our 23 community partners, four are considered to be “for profit”: • Two of those “for profit” entities are small family childcare centers. • One is a locally-based organization. • One is a national corporate provider. Cambridge Public Schools Department of Human Services Programs (DHSP) Community-Based Programs • Cambridge Public Schools currently offers CPP seats across 12 schools. • Program options include traditional preschool programs, Montessori programs, and language-immersion programs (Spanish, Portuguese, and Mandarin). • Preschool programming is also offered to children with special needs through a range of program options, including substantially separate classrooms and inclusion classrooms. • DHSP currently offers CPP seats across 7 sites. (For SY25-26, DHSP will offer CPP seats across 9 sites.) 7
Page 8 of 29
CPP is a family-choice-based system that enables each eligible family to select from a range of preschool options based on their individual family interests and needs. Who is eligible for CPP? • All four-year-olds who are Cambridge residents and who turn four by September 1 of each academic year. Four-year-olds from low-income families are prioritized for seats. • Some three-year-olds who turn three by September 1 of each academic year. Three-year-olds are prioritized if they meet the following criteria: How do families receive CPP seats? • CPP is a matching system that uses a single, centralized online application to allow for a simplified and streamlined process for families. (Rather than having to apply to multiple programs separately, families can apply to multiple programs through this single application.) • Families can select and rank up to 10 programs. • A matching algorithm matches families with available seats based on families’ selections and rankings. • Family is eligible for free/reduced lunch (for CPS). • Family income at or below 65% of area median income. • Family is currently experiencing homelessness. • Child is in foster care. Additionally, a limited number of three-year-old seats are available for non-priority students in CPS and DHSP classrooms. 8

How did we get here? 9
Page 10 of 29
CPP evolved from a longstanding commitment from city leaders to universal high- quality early childhood education, prioritizing children who have historically been furthest from access to high-quality preschool. • In 2015 the Cambridge Early Childhood Task Force issued its recommendations, which included a recommendation to create the “Birth to 3rd Grade (B-3) Partnership” – A city-wide collaborative focused on expanding access to and quality of early childhood programming across Cambridge, especially for lower-income families. • In 2017 the City Council committed to pursuing universal Pre-Kindergarten. The B-3 Partnership began researching various UPK models. • In 2019 the B-3 Partnership presented UPK model options to City Council and the School Committee during a Joint Roundtable. Following the Partnership’s recommendation, City Council Committed to following a mixed delivery model. The B-3 Partnership began strategic planning to design this new mixed delivery system. • The B-3 Partnership became the Office of Early Childhood (OEC) in 2022. Shortly thereafter, OEC began recruiting CPP program partners, and partnered with a software development firm to design the matching algorithm. • The CPP system launched in 2024: • Under the B-3 Partnership, a scholarship program was created that provided scholarships to 50 preschool-age children who met income-eligibility criteria. This was later expanded to serve 75 children. The Partnership also included program quality support services for early childhood programs across Cambridge, including coaching, leadership mentoring, and professional development. • Winter 2024 – The first match was implemented. • September 2024 – The first group of CPP children started school. • October 2024 –The second round of matching was implemented for SY25-26. • Early 2025 – Families will be notified of their matches for SY25-26. 10

The CPP Matching Process Applications are marked priority if the child/family is: • At or below 65% of AMI • Eligible for free or reduced lunch (for CPS) • Experiencing homelessness • In foster care Round 1 Matching Process Runs Priority applications are matched with available seats using Bridgecare Algorithm. (Siblings and continuity-of-care students have seats set aside for them.) Round 2 Matching Process Runs All remaining applications are then matched with available seats using the Bridgecare algorithm. Families choose from 6- 10 preschool programs and rank them in their application in order of preference. OEC, CPS, and DHSP staff have partnered with Bridgecare, a national software development firm focused on designing software solutions, including the matching algorithms, for early childhood providers. A note about the KLO closure… Consistent with the School Committee’s decision on December 17, 2024 to close the Kennedy Longfellow School (KLo), some Cambridge Public Schools preschool classrooms, which are part of CPP, will be housed at the Longfellow School (Broadway). OEC, CPS, and DHSP staff are working with a limited number of families who ranked KLo preschool classrooms as their top choice in the CPP match to ensure that these families can enroll in a preschool program that meets their interests and needs. CPS will continue working on the further development of preschool programming to enable greater instructional synergies and appropriate prioritization of early childhood education. 17

Upcoming Policy Decisions 18
Page 19 of 29
CPP is a complex system that is in very early stages of implementation. Year 1 of implementation is underway. Limited changes can be made for Year 2. What changes were made for Year 2 of the CPP match? • Based on feedback from programs and families, OEC shifted the match from the winter to the fall. • Because of the very limited time between the implementation of the SY24-25 match process and the implementation of the SY25-26 match, few significant structural changes could be made to the system. Changes that were made included the following: • More exhaustive community outreach including interactive media and application videos. • More detailed information about each preschool option is included in the application platform. Each program listing now includes detailed information about hours of operation (school day vs. extended day), curriculum approach, etc. to assist families in selecting programs that will best meet their interests and needs. • Application is translated into 5 languages. • Continuity of care status provided to current CPP 3-year-olds. • Daily availability (M-F) of in-person application support. • Application review, and opportunities for families to correct any application errors, omissions. 19

In order to ensure effective policy-making moving forward, OEC is committed to extensive stakeholder engagement and ongoing feedback from the community. What community / stakeholder engagement efforts have already taken place? • During the summer of 2024, OEC partnered with the Institute for Community Health to conduct an initial evaluation, including surveys and focus groups with CPP providers and families. • Since assuming the role of Executive Director in October, Dr. Ohlson has conducted multiple Preschool Provider Focus Groups in order to meet program directors from across the city and to gather feedback about what has been working well with CPP and related programming and what can be improved. • Dr. Ohlson is also in the process of visiting each CPP program in order to learn more about each preschool program community and about the broader early childhood landscape across the city. What community / stakeholder engagement efforts will be underway this winter and beyond? • OEC will be convening a preschool program CPP Advisory Board that will meet regularly and will be designed to provide ongoing feedback and input regarding key policy decisions. • Similarly, OEC will be convening a CPP Parent Advisory Board to ensure that parents / caregivers voices and perspectives are centered in our decision-making processes. • A “Key Decisions” group, comprised of leaders from OEC, DHSP, and CPS will convene regularly to align on programmatic decisions and craft CPP policy. • OEC leadership is meeting with a number of local and national program evaluation experts to design a comprehensive evaluation plan for SY25-26 and beyond. 20

As we refine the long-term vision of CPP, what significant policy issues will be addressed? 1 2 3 The supply of Pre-K seats currently exceeds demand. What is the impact of CPP on the preschool / childcare landscape, and what policy changes can be made to support a healthy preschool ecosystem? CPS programs currently do not offer extended day options. This is a challenge for families who prefer CPS preschool options that matriculate, but who need extended day care. Children with complex developmental needs currently have limited options in terms of preschool programs. As such, their families have few choices of preschool programming, and their options for extended day care are limited. 4 In order to elevate the field of early childhood educators and ensure high-quality programming in all CPP classrooms, OEC seeks to implement educator credential and pay parity requirements for CPP educators. This may cause financial strain for some programs. 21

Significant policy decisions will be made that will impact Year 3 implementation and beyond. There is currently an over-supply of preschool seats compared to population / demand. This is largely due to: • Declining birth rates: • Increasing number of preschool seats: ⮚Birth rates in MA have declined by 25% since 1990, with significant declines since 2016. ⮚Birth rates in Cambridge are among the lowest in the state. ⮚At least ten new preschool classrooms have opened in Cambridge since 2020. ⮚Next year, at least an additional 6 preschool classrooms will be opened. 22

Many preschool programs / childcare centers across the city are being negatively impacted by the over-supply of preschool seats. CPP has not created this challenge, but it plays a role by providing parents with more preschool choices, free of charge. ✔Some CPP preschool programs (as well as non-CPP programs across the city) are in greater demand from families than others. Those that have lower demand are experiencing low enrollment. ✔Low enrollment, or unfilled seats, creates financial strain for programs. ✔Because CPP is a choice-based system, families are choosing programs that best meet their interests and needs. Some types of programs (for example, those that do not offer full-day services) have been in less demand than other programs. 23

OEC’s long-term vision is that CPP is designed to support and promote a healthy preschool ecosystem across the city of Cambridge. In a healthy preschool ecosystem in Cambridge: ✔Quality of programming is generally consistent across programs: All preschool programs in Cambridge provide high-quality care and early learning experiences. ✔Cambridge families benefit from a robust network of locally operated, community-based preschools. ✔All children, regardless of family income, have access to a variety of preschool options. ✔There are a relatively small number of vacant preschool seats across programs; most programs are filled to capacity or are nearly full. ✔Programs that serve infants, toddlers, and preschoolers have robust preschool enrollment to offset the higher cost of infant/toddler care. ✔Children with developmental delays or disabilities have a range of programs to choose from that can provide specialized care and meet their needs. ✔CPP promotes equity and diversity across the system, such that children from low-income families are prioritized for preschool seats, and most preschool programs serve a population of children and families that generally reflects the diversity of the city. 24

OEC is studying the issue of preschool supply and demand to identify policy decisions that will support a healthy preschool ecosystem across Cambridge. Within five years, OEC aims to have identified and implemented policy measures designed to promote and sustain this ecosystem. Community Engagement and Policy-Making: • Conduct an in-depth analysis of the recently-finalized matching data and family surveys in order to identify and examine trends in family choices. • Conduct extensive community and stakeholder engagement, through the Family and Provider Advisory Boards and other mechanisms, to engage the community in this discussion and to ensure a diversity of perspectives, experiences, and viewpoints. • Identifying CPP policy decisions to be implemented in SY26-27 and beyond, that will move us towards a healthier early childhood ecosystem. This will include a determination about the criteria and process through which programs may be added to or removed from CPP. In the meantime, supporting preschool programs by: • Sharing information about preschool supply / demand across the city to enable programs to make strategic decisions about enrollment and program expansion. • Sharing information about trends in family interests and needs, based on match data, so that programs can explore programmatic changes that will align with families’ interests and needs. • Providing resources and support relating to small business administration (enrollment, financial planning).
