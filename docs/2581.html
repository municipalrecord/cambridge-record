That the attached Community Development Department recommendations for Inclusionary Zoning be forwarded to the full City Council with a favorable recommendation

Community Development Department – 8/11/2016 INCLUSIONARY HOUSING – FRAMEWORK FOR DRAFTING A ZONING PETITION Set-aside Ratio  Increase the inclusionary housing set-aside ratio from the current 15% before applying the density bonus (which typically yields a net 11-12% of units being affordable after applying the density bonus) to require a net 20% of units and/or floor area after applying the density bonus.  Maintain the current standard mirroring provisions so that 20% of floor area results in a net 20% of units being affordable, with unit mix mirroring the project as a whole (except for the alternative selection option as provided below).  Developers make a per-square-foot contribution to the Cambridge Affordable Housing Trust for required affordable floor area where the ratio results in “fractional units,” based on the cost of producing new affordable housing. Phasing-in of requirements  Increase the set-aside ratio to a net of 15% of total units or floor area at the time of adoption of new inclusionary housing provisions; 6 months from the date of adoption the set-aside ratio should increase to a net of 20% of total units or floor area. Income eligibility and affordability targeting  Increase the income eligibility limit for inclusionary homeownership units to 100% of area median income (AMI); raise income targeting for homeownership units to 90% AMI.  Maintain the income eligibility limit for inclusionary rental units at the current limit of 80% AMI, with rents based on 30% of tenant income.  For studio units, set housing prices at 25% of household income at the targeted affordability level so that studios are more affordable than one-bedroom units. Density Bonus  Maintain the current 30% FAR and unit density bonus.  Consider ideas for other zoning changes which would make the full 30% density bonus more attainable, such as limited relief from height, setback or parking requirements (pending input from housing developers). Creation of Family Units  Create an alternative compliance option to allow developers to increase the number of affordable three-bedroom and larger units while maintaining the affordability requirement at 20% of floor area. As a result, more family-size units could be created in exchange for fewer than 20% of the total unit count being affordable.  Consider limitations on building types or configurations which can use this option, and/or limits on how much of the affordable component could be designated for family-sized housing under this option.

Community Development Department – 8/11/2016 Threshold Project Size  Maintain the project size threshold of 10 units or 10,000 square feet.  Eliminate special permit approval currently required for voluntary inclusionary projects to incentivize creating affordable units in smaller projects.  Expand the definition of inclusionary projects to include existing residential properties of 10 or more units that are being restored after having been 100% vacant for a period of two or more years.
