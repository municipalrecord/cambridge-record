51 Tree Protection Ordinance - Takeaways Not all trees being removed under the Tree Protection Ordinance are being replanted. Half of the canopy trees which are removed are being replaced with subcanopy trees.
Page 52 of 66
52 TPO Recommendations: Replace more trees 75% of privately owned trees that are permitted for removal are considered "hazardous" and therefore do not require mitigation under the current ordinance. For “hazardous” trees: • Require 1:1 replacement so the forest continues to regenerate without being punitive by requiring caliper-inch mitigation. • Recommend that Green Cambridge plant replacement trees where cost is an issue for the owner.
Page 53 of 66
53 TPO Recommendations: Replace canopy with canopy In the analysis of the replacement trees from the last five years of TPO data, 50% of canopy trees are replaced with understory trees, which provide less cooling when mature. • Require at least one replacement tree for each removal be from the same size category and from a city-approved list of species, unless there is a significant lack of space as determined by the City Arborist. • Add language that tree species must be climate suitable and add recommendation on genetic diversification and nursery sourcing
Page 54 of 66
54 TPO Recommendations: Include ROW Trees Covered by Chapter 87 Protect critical root zones • Define a tree-protection zone buffer around existing street trees, including extending onto public sidewalks. Identify ROW trees in City applications • Show abutting trees within 30’ of the property boundary on Building Permits and Tree Protection Plan Protect all trees from over-pruning • Define a threshold for maximum allowable pruning of the tree’s canopy within a specified timeframe
Page 55 of 66
55 TPO Recommendations: Allow management of invasive trees In naturally regenerating areas such as Fresh Pond and Alewife Reservation, trees are grown tightly together. It is onerous for the City to comply with the TPO requirements when removing large invasive trees in these areas, especially since there is limited room to replant in those areas • For naturally regenerating areas, set a mitigation requirement of 1:1 replacement to facilitate restoration areas and removing of invasive species.

56 Green Factor 70 projects went through Green Factor between 2023 and 2025. Of those projects, 858 existing trees were retained, and 1,080 new trees were planted. At maturity, these projects will average 30% canopy cover. 18% Existing canopy cover 30% Canopy cover at maturity

57 Green Factor Recommendations • Broaden the applicability of the Green Factor requirements. • Review the weighting structure of different strategies (preserving existing canopy, planting new trees) to ensure that the structure is incentivizing tree preservation and planting. • Require / Verify compliance with minimum soil volumes for new trees. • Track and analyze the impact of the recent zoning change that converted all residential to C-1 zoning classification. • Require during the certificate of occupancy approvals that the design is reviewed for compliance in terms of the number and types of trees planted.
