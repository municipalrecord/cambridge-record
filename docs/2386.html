Attachment E 11 July, 2016 [address removed] Cambridge City Council Housing Committee City Hall, 2nd Floor 795 Massachusetts Avenue Cambridge, MA 02139 Re: Inclusionary Housing Study; Recommendations of the Cambridge Affordable Housing Trust To the members of the Cambridge City Council Housing Committee, The Trust is recommending an increase in the income eligibility limit for inclusionary homeownership to 100% of area median income (AMI), which is $78,500 for a two-person household, and $88,300 for a three-person household. We are also recommending that the income limit for the inclusionary rental housing program be kept at 80% of AMI, which is $54,960 for a one-person household. (More than half of tenants in Cambridge's inclusionary rental housing program are one-person households.) It is important to know the reasons for the making these recommendations: - First, that is where the demand, the overwhelming demand, is for affordable housing. - Second, the dramatic change in income demographics in Cambridge since the 1990s. - Third, the housing cost burdens experienced by lower income renter households in Cambridge. This spring, 408 applications were included in the lottery for twelve new tax credit units at the Port Landing Apartments. (Low Income Housing Tax Credit units have an income limit of 60% of AMI.) The 738 applications that were submitted for eight lower income units (at 30% and 50 of AMI) were ineligible for the lottery. CHA also sent the CDD additional names of applicants from the public housing wait list and public housing tenants who need to relocate. By contrast, there were only 69 applications for 15 affordable middle income inclusionary rental units at the Alexandria development, only 43 of which were income eligible (22 of the 25 that didn't meet income limits were below income). The point is that it was a lot harder to fill the higher middle income units (at 100 - 120 % of AMI). As of the end of June, one unit was still available. The developers that CDD reached out to for feedback on the inclusionary study were receptive to the idea of including a middle-income component, however, they said that in practical terms, a higher income set-aside would be less advantageous if the units are slower to rent which has been the case in recent projects. As stated in the DRA study, there is a demand for affordable middle income homeownership units, particularly, smaller bedroom sized units. It is worth mentioning that the city's Financial Assistance Program has been very successful in helping Cambridge households with incomes above 80% of AMI (that are currently ineligible for inclusionary housing) purchase one, two, and three-bedroom units across the city's neighborhoods. 1

You are already aware that there has been a dramatic change in income demographics in Cambridge. The DRA study looked at the period of 2000 - 2011. There was a decrease in households in the 50 - 100% AMI range and an increase in households above 120% AMI. According to a CDD analysis of incomes in Cambridge since 1990, the number of households in Cambridge with incomes in the 50 to 80% AMI range declined by almost 49 % between 1990 and 2011. The great majority of lower income renter households in Cambridge are housing cost burdened (paying more than 30% of their income for housing). Over half of these households are severely housing cost burdened (paying over 50 % of AMI). If you are one of the lucky few who get a voucher, the trade-off is that you might have to leave Cambridge to use it. The CHA's 2016 Moving to Work (MTW) Annual Report states that 43% of CHA MTW tenant-based voucher households live outside of Cambridge. This represents an increase of 12% over the past five years. An increasing number of voucher households, both inside and outside of Cambridge, are housing cost burdened. The inclusionary program is serving households that would otherwise be at risk of displacement. Currently, 53 % of inclusionary rental units are occupied by tenants with mobile vouchers. In this high cost city, lower income people who are housed in inclusionary units do not have to choose between leaving Cambridge, and giving up the option of mobility. Sincerely, Cheryl-Ann Pizza-Zeal Cheryl-Ann Pizza-Zeoli Trustee
