McGovern, Chair of the Human Services and Veterans Committee, for a public hearing held on May 18, 2016 to discuss the opioid crisis in Cambridge with representatives from the Human Services Department and the Cambridge Police Department as well as local providers.

OPEN Cambridge Everett Somerville Watertown Overdose Prevention & Education Network OPEN is a local collaboration to address opioid abuse and overdose. The Overdose Prevention and Education Network, OPEN, is a regiona l collaboration between the communities of Cambridge, Everett, Somerville and Watertown, Massachusetts to address the issue of opioid use and abuse and 'fatal and non-fatal opioid overdoses. OPEN is working with community members and organizations, local hospitals, police, fire, and health departments across all four towns to raise awareness about prescription opioid safety and prevent fatal and non-fatal overdoses. This coalition includes AIDS Action Committee, the fire and police departments, Cambridge Health Alliance, CASPAR/ BayCove, ProEMS, CRLS, Cambridge Department of Human Service Programs, and more. What has been the effect of the opioid crisis on Cambridge? o According to our 2013 Community Health Assessment, alcohol and other substance abuse ranked as the #3 social and economic issue in Cambridge (after lack of affordable housing and homelessness). o Fatal and non-fatal overdoses are increasing over t ime all over the country, including here. They happen most frequently among men, 45-60 year olds, and white non-Hispanic individuals. o According to interviews with key informants, hotspots include Harvard and Central Squares and restaurants/public restrooms; often, non-Cambridge residents come here because it is a hub for substance abuse treatment and support services, as well as homeless services. o Heroin addiction can happen to anyone. 4 out of 5 times, em opioid addiction began with legal or recreational use of prescription drugs that transitioned to heroin because of a change in availability or price. ' What are OPEN's strategies to reduce addiction and overdose? o Providing up-to-date information to t he community about stigma, prevention, intervention, and treatment. ~ The OPEN website (www.odprevention.org) and Twitter (@OPENCambridge) are updated daily with local news, safety tips, and resources. Gl Encouraging residents to safely store and dispose of medications when they are no longer needed, including distributing literature to residents about medication safety and disposal events in their neighborhood. ~ CPD has collected more than 25,000 lbs of medications since 2010; lobby kiosk collection has grown almost 14x since 2010. Increasing awareness of prescription risks by creating a brochure el(plaining prescription misuse for prescribers and pharmacists to explore with their patients. ~ The brochure is included in the Mt. Other Awareness Efforts o The OPEN Twitter (@OPENCambridge) was launched in August 2015 with the intended purpose of engaging Cambridge audiences in reducing opioid use and overdose and building an online relationship with our partners. o On October 5, 2015, the Mayor's Office organized a roundtable discussion about opioids in Cambridge. Stakeholders gathered to comment on the opioid response in Cambridge, including the work done by the public health, healthcare, and first responder sectors. OPEN gave a 15-minute presentation about our purpose, t he epidemiology of the opioid epidemic, and our current and future strategies. o The Recovery Coach Pilot is a collaboration between several Cambridge partners, led by ProEMS, that aims to help high utilizers of emergency services better coord inate and navigate addiction treatment systems and recovery. o OPEN was featured at the December 2015 Cambridge Public Health Subcommittee (quasi board of health) meeting, and gave a presentation about our mission, strategies, and plans for the future. What are OPEN's next steps? o Educating student athletes and their parents about non-medical use of prescription drugs (student athletes are at higher risk of addiction because they may be prescribed painkillers when injured). o Publicizing that naloxone is available "behind-the counter" at CVS and Walgreens pharmacies, and advocating that all customers receive the correct dosage and training when they purchase it. o Continuing to promo'i:e the disposal of unwanted medication, including a 24/7 kiosk in the Cambridge Police Station lobby, four DPW hazardous waste days (April-October), and pre-paid medication disposal envelopes. o Distributing our new "Prevent Prescription Misuse" brochure that pharmacists and prescribers can use as an educational tool with patients. How can I help? o Take a pre-paid medication envelope and safely and easily dispose of all your old, unwanted medications. o Talk to your children about prescription drug safety and the link between Rx painkillers and heroin. o Talk to your children's coach about the warning signs of painkiller addiction and how to prevent it. o Refer to 11Learn to Cope" which is a free, anonymous, and safe support group for parents and families of people with a drug addiction. They meet every Monday at 7pm at Spaulding Hospital and naloxone is available at all meet ings. o Go to your local pharmacy and ask for naloxone. Help us to create a safer and stigma-free community !
