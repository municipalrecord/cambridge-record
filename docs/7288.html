First Street Area Parking Planning Study v Kleinfelder and McMahon Associates performed the parking study v This study evaluates the potential effects that leasing 420 additional unassigned parking spaces in the garage could have on parking demand in the area, now and in the future. 8
Page 9 of 17
Parking Study Conclusions • There is ample parking within the study area to accommodate the anticipated 336 new daily parkers (based on 80% utilization of the 420 parking passes) at all times of day; • The proposed lease will not inhibit the City's ability to provide monthly parking passes and parking spaces during declared snow emergencies to Cambridge residents; • Parking supply within the study area is significantly higher than parking demand, even if parking capacity at the CambridgeSide Mall is reduced in the future; and • The proposed lease should not impact the availability of residential parking within the neighborhood, since non- resident customers parking within the garage will not be able to access on-street resident permit parking. 9 Based on these conclusions, TPT Director Joe Barr recommends that the City Manager move forward with the disposition process.

Other Considerations on the Courthouse project • The City does not own the Courthouse. It is owned and managed by the Commonwealth of Massachusetts' Division of Capital and Asset Management and Maintenance (DCAMM) • DCAMM has reiterated repeatedly that the Courthouse is a valuable asset and that it expects to obtain fair market value in any disposition of this property. • Site Acquisition cost of at least $30M, Environmental Remediation estimated by LMP to be at least $50M. Minimum $80M to acquire and remediate the property. • The Courthouse has been vacant since 2014, has a number of deteriorating conditions, is unsuitable for habitation, and is a blight on the East Cambridge neighborhood. • Additional Benefits required by already approved Special permit include: – Environmental remediation and decrease in height of Courthouse – New Public Open Space – $7M Incentive Zoning payments to Affordable Housing Trust – Estimated new annual property tax revenue of $3.8M 13

MOST SIGNIFICANT BENEFITS Of Leasehold interest in the First Street Garage 14

15 • Proposed lease will produce the most significant public benefit that can be obtained from the proposed disposition, specifically in context of already permitted Courthouse project. • $77.4 million in economic benefits through disposition of leasehold interest, including: – 24 fully affordable housing units, $4.5 million for the affordable housing trust. Guaranteed parking fees and retail lease payments of $52.7 million over 30 years. – Result in activation of the first floor retail & capital improvements for garage equaling $5.28 million. – Contributions to community workforce development totaling $1.45 million over ten years with Cambridge Pathways to Apprentice Program, Just-a-Start, Cambridge Housing Authority & Community Benefits Stabilization Fund Contribution. – Creation of community space, senior space ($750,000) and senior parking subsidies at garage, up to $1,080,000. – Updating the garage to accommodate charging for 40 electric vehicles and a solar array supporting the City Council goals of sustainable energy and environmental resilience, $5.65 million.

16 • Approving this lease will benefit the East Cambridge businesses that have suffered since the Courthouse closed. • Estimated annual new property tax revenues $3.8 million. • An additional $7 million in Incentive Zoning as part of the Special Permit. • The First Street garage was built in part to support the Courthouse. • There is adequate space to accommodate both the lease and Cambridge residents based on professional & independent Parking Study. • The approval of this lease will allow for the remediation of the environmental contamination and redevelop a blighted building in the East Cambridge neighborhood. • Community benefits and amenities that will be provided to East Cambridge by proposed disposition of the leasehold interest at the First Street Garage as well as by the redevelopment of the Courthouse by LMP will positively impact the community for years.

In Conclusion 17 I recommend that the City Council approve the disposition of the Leasehold Interest and authorize me to enter into and execute a Disposition Agreement with LMP for the Leasehold Interest. I strongly urge that a two-thirds majority of the Council vote in favor of and agree with the Planning Board’s recommendation that the proposed disposition moves forward. I want to thank the City Council for its leadership during this process.
