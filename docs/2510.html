City of Cambridge Executive Department Lisa C. Peterson • Deputy City Manager Richard C. Rossi • City Manager To: Richard Rossi, City Manager Date: September 15, 2016 Re: Allocation and Appropriation of FY2017 CPA Funds The purpose of this memo is to summarize the process of the Community Preservation Act Committee (CPAC) and to convey its recommendations made on September 7, 2016. Recommendations were based on the City's goals, staff presentations and public comments heard at the CPA public meetings held on June 15, 2016 and July 28, 2016, public input from letters, emails, online project requests and petitions received since the beginning of the process, and CPAC discussions at working CPAC meetings on May 19, 2016 and September 7, 2016. At the June 15, 2016 public meeting, nine members of the public spoke about preserving long-term affordability at Fresh Pond Apartments and other expiring use properties, continued support of the First-Time Homebuyers Program, work on projects that address the Fair Housing Act and homelessness, receiving preservation grant funds to support restoration of church stain glass windows. One petition signed by 31 tenants from Fresh Pond Apartments was submitted. A public hearing was held on July 28, 2016, to discuss the allocation preferences among the three CPA funding categories. Eighteen of the 20 people that spoke at this meeting advocated for the CPAC to continue the 80% allocation to affordable housing. Five separate petitions with 189 signatures were submitted supporting affordable housing along with 4 requests supporting funding interpretive signage at Magazine Beach and rehabilitation work at Lowell Memorial Park. It was also noted during the CPAC discussions, that in addition to CPA funds, many open space projects throughout the city are funded from a variety of other sources, including the appropriation of over $30 million from bonds, free cash, other city funding, and private mitigation funds, since FY11. Public comments received through September 7, 2016 included: • Nine petitions with a total of 593 signatures in favor of Affordable Housing. • Forty-five individual requests were received in total: • 16 supported affordable housing projects • 11 supported the 80% allocation for affordable housing • 4 supported the interpretive signage at Magazine Beach • 3 supported the rehabilitation of Magazine Beach Powder House • 5 supported the rehabilitation of Lowell Memorial Park • 6 supported a variety of projects related to open space and historic preservation.
