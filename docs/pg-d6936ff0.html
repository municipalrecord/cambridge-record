March 24, 2026 To the Honorable, the City Council: When I came into this role in 2022, there had never been a performance review for the City Manager. Working with the Council to stand up a rigorous and transparent performance review process for the City Manager was one of my first priorities. 2026 will be the fourth year that we are conducting this process. I’m grateful for the partnership with the City Council, and the privilege of serving in this role. Doing this well is an important foundation for trust in our local government, and I have found each year to include constructive feedback and substantive discussions. The process over the last three years has provided good structure, and I would propose that we follow a similar overall template. The original rating categories were developed based on the City Council’s 2022 City Manager Leadership Profile. However, the biggest area of feedback has been that the goals and rating categories focused only on the administrative side of the role without weighing enough the major areas of work that the City Council and the City Administration focus on: Housing and Zoning, Economic Opportunity and Equity, Transportation, Sustainability and Climate Resilience, and Government and Council Performance. This year, in discussion with the Mayor and Chair of Government Operations, I propose that we consolidate rating categories to five areas that cover City Administration: Leadership and Management, City Operations, Fiscal Management, Community Engagement, and Culture and Anti-racism, Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion. Separately, I propose two additional areas that cover Council Collaboration: Council Priorities, and Council Relationship. City Administration covers the executive work of the City Manager to run a large, public facing organization that must deliver on day-to-day services, establish trust with the community, manage short and long-term strategy, and ensure financial performance, organizational strength, and a positive and effective culture. Council Collaboration covers the governance work of the City Manager, to build a collaborative and trusting working relationship with the City Council, and work together to execute on key priorities. On goals, my reflection over the last three years has been that we have had three different types of goals: 1) highlighted elements of the job that we were leaning into, 2) key initiatives with milestones, and 3) measures with target performance metrics. At this point, it has felt like highlighted elements such as engaging on regional priorities or incorporating greater Council

engagement in the budget process are not as important to include as goals. These are accepted parts of the role. Capturing more clearly then, goals around key initiatives and measures with target performance metrics feels more clear. The other major feedback on goals from the City Council is to focus more on the work that we do together across Council Priorities. My struggle thus far has been recognizing that this gets closer to the most important work we do together, and yet has been challenging to incorporate into this process. First, our collective agenda is set together, and often isn’t fully decided in the first part of the calendar year in time for this process. Second, while the City Manager plays a key role, the City Council is necessarily a key driver of both how we prioritize and in many cases such as the work we have done on zoning, a key player in the work. Finally, I recognize that the City Council does not have one view, but many – and that success and failure may be defined very differently by different councillors. I would hold up the work on Housing and Zoning during the 2024-2025 Council term as the example that we may aspire toward. We entered the term with substantive initiatives not yet completed, including zoning reform for North Mass Ave, Cambridge Street, and a proposal to re- zoning Central Square. We ultimately landed a re-prioritization of work with the City Council in Spring 2024 that set clear timelines and fit within the capacity of City staff. After this workplan was completed, we re-visited our prioritization in Spring 2025, and aligned on a new set of work that brought us through the end of 2025, with the final zoning for Cambridge Street passing in early 2026. We recently brought through a third workplan on Housing and Zoning in March, and are now set on a new set of initiatives. The City Council, through the Finance Co-chairs, is now also undergoing a prioritization process that will cover potential work across the Council priority areas. My hope is that we would similarly bring forward to the City Council a prioritized workplan for each of the other areas. Success would mean that we have found consensus on the City Council, alignment with key department leadership and staff, and clarity on high-level scope, impact, cost, staff resources, and timeline. I’m hopeful that this will provide us a structure and process for alignment, and also a way to better communicate to our community the work that we see as most important – and whether we are producing results. Very truly yours, Yi-An Huang City Manager
