C I T Y O F C A M B R I D G E Community Development Department April 17, 2019 Page 1 of 6 IRAM FAROOQ Assistant City Manager for Community Development SANDRA CLARKE Deputy Director Chief of Administration KHALIL MOGASSABI Deputy Director Chief of Planning TO: Louis A. DePasquale, City Manager FROM: Iram Farooq, Assistant City Manager for Community Development SUBJECT: Accessory Apartments – Alternative Zoning Text DATE: April 17, 2019 The Planning Board has held two hearings, the first on January 22, 2019 and the second on March 26, 2019, and each time reviewed a different version of the City Council’s Zoning Petition to Amend Section 4.22 “Accessory Apartments” (the “Petition”). The Board made recommendations on the Petition, and also recommended that the City Council consult with City staff, including the Community Development Department and Law Department, to develop language. The suggested zoning text on the following pages is intended to apply the substantive recommendations made by the Planning Board to the Petition. The Board supported the following specific changes:  Removing the condition prohibiting significant enlargements of the dwelling since built  Removing the condition requiring a minimum lot size of 5,000 square feet  Removing the prohibition on accessory apartments in an existing garage  Removing the owner occupancy requirement  Removing existing language pertaining to off-street parking requirements and continuing to not require accessory parking for an accessory apartment  Exempting the accessory apartment from the lot area per dwelling unit calculation As recommended by the Planning Board, the suggested text is intended to clarify the language in the Petition, to make the language consistent with other provisions of the Zoning Ordinance, and to avoid unintended problems in its implementation. This suggested version uses terminology consistent with definitions and language used elsewhere in the Zoning Ordinance, whereas the terminology used in the Petition is somewhat inconsistent and could lead to unintended confusion in its implementation. This suggested text is also structured to more clearly separate and delineate:  what is allowed,  what are the limitations on what is allowed,  what specific relief may be granted by the BZA, and  what are the criteria for BZA approval. Finally, this version intends to resolve conflicts with other sections of the Zoning Ordinance, namely the provisions in Section 4.21 that expressly prohibit the conversion of an accessory building to a dwelling unit.

An accessory use with one or more rooms with separate kitchen and bathroom facilities, constituting a dwelling unit, located within on the same lot and under the same ownership as a single or two family detached dwelling and designed for the occupancy of a single family. In ARTICLE 4.000 – USE REGULATIONS, amend Paragraphs j. and m. below Section 4.21 “Accessory Uses” as follows: j. No accessory building shall be used as a dwelling except in an Industrial District for the accommodation of a night watchman or janitor or in any district in accordance with the requirements for Accessory Apartments as set forth in Section 4.22. k. An accessory building in Residence A, B, C, C-1, and Office-1 districts shall not exceed fifteen (15) feet in height above the ground level. l. No accessory building may be converted to a residential use unless it conforms with the district dimensional regulations specified in Section 5.30 or with the requirements for Accessory Apartments as set forth in Section 4.22. In ARTICLE 4.000 – USE REGULATIONS, amend Section 4.22 “Accessory Apartments” as follows: 4.22 Accessory Apartments. The purpose of this Subsection 4.22 is to allow for the creation of accessory apartments in all districts. Many large existing single and two-family homes dwellings or other accessory buildings on their lots are underutilized. Alteration of these homes buildings to provide one additional dwelling units on the lot would be prohibited in most cases due to the existing floor area ratio and/or lot area per dwelling unit requirements of Subsection 5.31, among other zoning limitations. Given contemporary lifestyles, housing needs, and energy and maintenance costs, it is beneficial to the City to allow greater flexibility in the use of such dwellings buildings to add new dwelling units without substantially altering the environmental quality of their surrounding neighborhoods. This Subsection 4.22 gives the Board of Zoning aAppeal the authority to relax such requirements in certain instances as enumerated below. 4.22.1 In all districts the Board of Zoning Appeal may grant a special permit for the alteration of an existing single- family detached dwelling, or two-family dwelling, or accessory building on the same lot as a single-family or two-family detached dwelling to provide one accessory apartment if the following conditions are met: 1. The building containing the accessory apartment has been in existence since on or before February 1, 2019. 1. The dwelling has not been substantially enlarged since built. The addition in the aggregate of two hundred and fifty (250) square feet or more of gross floor area shall be considered a substantial enlargement.
