Inclusionary Housing Proposal from Cambridge Residents Alliance, 5/18/16 The Cambridge Residents Alliance (CResA) has previously supported increasing the required amount of inclusionary housing from 11.5% of the total units to 20% for low and moderate income households, and 5% for middle income households. David Paul Rosen & Associates (DRA), the consultant who wrote Cambridge's Inclusionary Housing study, made recommendations in some areas, and suggested that the city council hold discussions in other areas. We are revising our position to take into account the information provided in the study, and are proposing specific positions on the major points covered by the study. 1. Threshold for required inclusionary units: 6 units. (The DRA study said that projects with 6 or more units could support an inclusionary unit.) 2. Percent of inclusionary units is based on number of units in project: • Require 6-9 unit projects to provide 15% inclusionary units • Require 10-15 unit projects to provide 20% inclusionary units • Require projects with over 15 units to provide 25% inclusionary units This sliding scale takes into account the findings of the DRA study that a 25% inclusionary requirement is economically feasible for larger developments but is not economically feasible for smaller developments. We agree with the study finding that the density bonus does not need to be increased. 3. Require payment for fractional units. 4. Inclusionary requirement for projects with over 15 units % of total Rental Unit Income Level For Sale Unit Income Level % of total units units Tier 1 20% 15% 50 to 80% AMI; target 65% * 50 to 80% AMI; target 65% Tier 2 2.5% 6% ** 80 to 100% AMI; target 90% 80 to 100% AMI; target 90% Tier 3 2.5% 4% 100 to 120% AMI; target 110% 100 to 120% AMI; target 110% Total 25% 25% * AMI= Area Median Income. "Target" means that all the households in the income range will on average equal the target. Having a target prevents all the households being at the upper end of the income range. ** We recommend a total of 10% of inclusionary units for middle income homeowners because we want to encourage middle income families to buy and settle in Cambridge. We propose 6% for tier 2 of homeownership units because the study indicates that this income range has more difficulty finding affordable housing than does the highest tier. 5. Require that 20% of inclusionary units be 3BRs; use square feet to calculate the reduction in the total number of inclusionary units. No requirement for market-rate 3BRs. 6. No inclusionary micro-units or studios allowed, as suggested by the consultant. 7. We do not address the issue of comparability of unit location at this time. We think the best way to address it would be to hold a conversation with people who are waiting for inclusionary housing and people who already have it.
