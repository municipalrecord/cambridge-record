CRT 2016 #39 · Agenda item attachment · Aug 1 2016

A communication was received from Paula M. Crane, Deputy City Clerk, transmitting a report from Councillor Craig Kelley, Chair of the Public Safety Committee and Mayor E. Denise Simmons and Vice Mayor Marc C. McGovern, Co-Chairs of the Housing Committee, for a joint public hearing held on July 19, 2016 to discuss the presence and impact of short-term rental units (Airbnb, FlipKey, VRBO, etc.) in Cambridge, and to hear suggestions from community members and operators on how best to address the challenges of this emerging market.

Results ‣10% increase in Airbnb listings corresponds to a decrease in hotel revenue of about 0.4% ‣This translates to ~10% decrease in hotel revenue in Austin, where Airbnb supply is highest ‣ Primarily driven by a reduction in price (~8%) and less so by changes in occupancy rate ‣An increase in Airbnb supply has about 1/4 the negative impact of a corresponding increase in hotel room supply ‣ Price tier: Budget and Economy hotels are the most aﬀected Midprice, Upscale, and Luxury properties less aﬀected ‣ Business travel: Hotels without business facilities, like meeting and conference space, are more aﬀected by Airbnb

So what? Hotels are likely underestimating Airbnb ‣ Zero marginal cost of new inventory allows Airbnb to scale Diminishes hotel pricing power ‣ Airbnb less regulated (taxation, zoning, insurance) Lower costs, prices ‣ High-degree of personalization and better online experience than most hotel websites ‣ Airbnb now targeting business customers too ‣ Hotels/OTAs diversifying by investing in “sharing economy” companies Travelers: win-win situation ‣ More choices ‣ Even travelers not using Airbnb beneﬁt from decreased hotel prices overall ‣ …and smaller price hikes during periods of peak demand Online travel agents ‣ OTAs are also threatened by Airbnb — Expedia/Homeaway, TripAdvisor/FlipKey Cities and municipalities ‣ Travel can be a signiﬁcant contributor to municipal/state revenue ‣ Airbnb is generating new travel revenue for cities… ‣ …but cities can lose if taxing Airbnb hosts is more challenging than taxing hotels
