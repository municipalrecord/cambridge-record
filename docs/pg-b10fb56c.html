Communication and Report from Other City Officers · ICE Tracker from CPD questions

A communication was received from Councillor Al-Zubi transmitting the City of Cambridge Welcoming Community Ordinance and a document related to the effects of ICE in the community.

The answers are from CET outreach workers and members of the Bangla, Haitian-Creyol, Amharic and Spanish-speaking community. 1. How has the current environment around immigration enforcement affected how you or people you know interact with city services, local police, or public spaces? • People from my community are scared to go to work. • People from my community don’t want to apply to any programs. They are scared to go to the supermarket, work and send kids to school. • I don’t know anyone who experiences this situation form my community. I think most of our people have green cards or student active visas. • I know that ICE was in Harvard one month ago now my son and his friend called me to tell my son to be careful. • People in my community are fearful of immigration enforcement, particularly those with TPS status. As a result, they limit their movements and go out when it is essential. I don’t know anyone who interacts with local police • The current immigration enforcement has affected my community in a way where they are more cautious them before. While they are going to work, school, however other activities they are no longer participating in due to fear of ICE. • The current situation around immigration enforcement affected my community even citizens are scared to go outside without passport, they fear ICE harassment. • I don’t have any, but people are scared and uncomfortable doing everything. • My community is scared about ICE. They need information about services if they encounter with ICE. My daughter that goes to CRLS asked me about ICE • It has been hard for people to trust the environment and leave their house for shopping or whatever to do outside their house. People are not attending events or not feeling comfortable interacting with each other like they used to. • The current situation around Immigration affects a lot of people from my community. Many of my community members that came to Cambridge from the Biden Parole Program or by crossing the border are living in fear if they get caught by the police or by Ice, they will get deported and separated from their loved ones (kids, husband, wife, sibling friend.).People limit their activities to the minimum. They will go out when necessary. People are more willing to participate in an event online them in person. They avoid going to any big gathering or even to church. Recently I was connected to a woman that is sick, does not have food and no source of income, but she won’t go to seek care or anything because of fear of ICE

Last month people saw ICE Agent in the parking lot of the ST John catholic church, at Rindge Ave and some other places in north Cambridge. That makes people very cautious in their move. • Someone said he is afraid about this issue. He said that he does not want to seek help from the police or any agency because sometimes they think it may cause problems with their immigration status. They are also scared to go to gathering places like community events or restaurants. They worry about sending their children to school. They feel that life is becoming harder now. • They said they have not personally faced anything like this, but they have heard about such situations. One person said that their friend went to the police to solve a problem but then faced many difficulties. The police verified their identity and found issues related to their immigration status. • They are not going to public places because of fear of being arrested by ICE, furthermore they are somewhat afraid of the police because they think they are somewhat connected. • This family was telling me that every time they go outside, they enter in panic, they walk looking around because they feel like ICE is behind them watching them following them at the same time not to have documents make them feel fear. • Its feels scared to go out and don’t know if you comeback at night I think so much about my kids when they are in the school , I read police are not involved with ICE but I believe its intimidating see any law enforcement I don’t want to apply for foods stamps I prefer go to the pantries but I avoid lines . • I try to avoid public transportation because even if I don’t see ICE around, I’m scared nobody is safe I basically spend my salary on Lyft, rent and food I avoid places with a lot of people • The current environment around immigration enforcement affected me, but not really on a deep level. I was mostly worried that the immigration officers wouldn't be handled by the law because, after all, it felt like they weren't following it. That was scary. But since there weren't any major activities around my neighborhood, I was kind of at ease when it came down to it. • The current environment around immigration enforcement has created a climate where many people feel unsafe and fearful engaging in their daily lives. This fear affects how individuals interact with city services, local police, and even public spaces. Many immigrants, including those with legal status such as green card holders, have become more cautious in their routines. There is hesitation in accessing services, traveling, or being in public places due to concerns about potentially encountering immigration agents and not wanting to take that risk. • This has been especially challenging for immigrant communities, as it creates ongoing stress and uncertainty. People often take extra precautions to avoid situations that could place them in a vulnerable position or potentially lead to separation from their families.

The impact is not limited to individuals without status; even those who are in the process of adjusting their status or who are legally present still experience fear and anxiety. This environment places an emotional and psychological burden on families and communities, making everyday activities feel risky and increasing overall stress. • The current environment around immigration enforcement has made many people afraid to interact with city services and local police. Some people avoid seeking help from the police because they fear being reported to the immigration enforcement. There is also a tendency to avoid going out to public spaces due to the fear of being stopped or arrested. This fear affects life and reduces trust in public institutions. • Someone said I feel a sense of apprehension, no longer feel as free as it used to be. • Another person, who is already a citizen, said he feels anxious about getting harassed by ICE. Not in this city but it is really scary to watch the news and see how aggressive they are and even if Cambridge protects the residents, they can only do so much. I hope we don’t have to see that kind of interaction in the city. 2. Have you or someone you know had an experience where local police or a city agency played a role, even indirectly, in an immigration enforcement situation? What happened, and what should the city have done differently • The city of Cambridge is super welcoming, and they always try to help all community members. No one among the people I served had any interaction with ICE. • People from my community who are not educated are scared. Even they have green card. They heard from other communities that make them worry • No Community members have shared any experience with ICE • People in the community are scared because they are not sure if police work with ICE or not even if the police said that they don’t So it’s a hard and sensitive situation right now. • I know Someone he and one of his friend walking at Rindge Ave in Cambridge saw ICE one shows his ID and passport, but the friends couldn’t show any ID, so ICE took him in their car. I don’t know what happened after it happened. • I don’t have any report from my community in Cambridge that they have any interaction with police nor ICE. People heard in the news what is happening in other states, even in some cities close to Cambridge. If the city is doing something to prevent ICE chasing people in the community until now it is working. • Community members want more info on what to do and the attitude when encounter with police or ICE Agents. • Secure food, medical care for those who lose or lose their work permit if TPS end. • Another person said that their friend saw ICE, and ICE officers verified his identity. ICE took his friend into their car, but after he showed his ID and green card, they let him go.

In my experience, they have focused on their duties, and I haven't seen anything they should have done differently regarding those situations. • While I have not had a personal experience myself, I have heard concerning stories from others in different states. In some situations, individuals were asked for identification and then taken in for questioning by immigration agents, even though they were U.S. citizens. These individuals were reportedly held for hours, asked extensive questions, and later released without assistance, leaving them to find their own way back. • Experiences like these can be distressing and create fear within communities. Situations where people are detained or questioned despite being citizens can feel traumatic and undermine trust in local institutions. In these cases, local police and city agencies should prioritize clear communication, ensure proper verification before detaining individuals, and avoid actions that may unnecessarily traumatize people. Policies that protect individuals’ rights and promote respectful, careful engagement would help reduce fear and build greater trust within the community • Thank God, I have not had a personal experience involving someone close to me in an immigration enforcement situation. However, I believe it would be a good idea for local police to clearly communicate that they are not involved with immigration enforcement agents. This could help reduce fear and build trust within the community • I didn’t find any answers for these questions, but one person mentioned that if anything happened to anyone (don’t share with others)
