BRIAN PFEIFFER PRESERVATION ADVISORY SERVICES Submitted electronically and via hand delivery May 17, 2016 Charles Sullivan, Executive Director Cambridge Historical Commission 831 Massachusetts Avenue Cambridge, Massachusetts 02139 RE: Ely Club, 2 Holyoke Place - Amendment to a Preservation Restriction Agreement Dear Charles: Enclosed please find a copy of an "Amended and Restated Historic Preservation Restriction" for the Fly Club which I submit on behalf of Preservation Massachusetts, Inc. (PMI) and Two Holyoke Place LLC for approval by the City of Cambridge. As you are aware, the building is currently subject to a Preservation Restriction that runs for a term of thirty years from its date of execution (December 22, 1997). As the existing Agreement covers only exterior features, and as both organizations wish to include interior features within the scope of the Restriction, they have entered into the attached Amendment which has been reviewed by the Massachusetts Historical Commission (MHC) and which has been modified to reflect conditions that MHC placed upon its approval. I have included a draft approval form for the Cambridge City Council within the document. I prepared this approval in the form that MHC prefers with provision for the notarized signature of each City Councilor. Some communities with which I work prefer to substitute their own Iorm in which the Mayor or other member of the City Council signs to attest to the vote in favor of the Restriction. I suspect that the City's Legal Department may have its own format for such approvals. For this reason, I have not numbered the pages of the photographs that follow as Exhibit C, lest they need to be re-numbered due to the use of an approval form different from the one I have attached. Could you let me know what, if any, additional information you may need to process this request? Also, if you would find it easier to have electronic files of this Agreement, please let me know and I will make them available to you. Sincerely yours, Brian Pfeiffer [address removed], Cambridge, Massachusetts 02138• [phone removed]

WITNESSETH: WHEREAS, Grantor represents that it is the owner of record in fee simple of certain real property located at Two Holyoke Place in the City of Cambridge and County of Middlesex, Commonwealth of Massachusetts, being the property described in Exhibit A attached hereto and incorporated herein by this reference, and also being the property shown on that certain plan entitled, "Plan of Land in Cambridge, Massachusetts," drawn by MassBay Survey Inc., Newton, Massachusetts, dated December 1984, and recorded with Middlesex County South District Registry of Deeds as Plan 1600 of 1984 as recited in deed of Ronald I. Lyman, Jr., et al., Trustees to Charles S. Cheston, Jr., Trustee, dated December 28, 1984 and recorded with said Deeds, Book 15949, page 164, a copy of which is attached hereto and incorporated herein as Exhibit B, and being that property conveyed to Grantor in a deed from The Fly Club Corporation, dated June 30, 1998, recorded with Middlesex County South District Registry of Deeds, Book 28787, page 23: The Fly Club (hereinafter "the Building"), is a wood-frame and brick structure of notable Colonial Revival style design composed of three sections. Built in 1896 from designs by Herbert D. Hale, the Main Block (hereinafter "Main Block") was a two-storey rectangular structure with brick end walls (north and south), clapboard-covered east and west elevations, and a pitched roof. In 1902, this arrangement was modified to the Building's present appearance by the addition of brick veneer to the east and west elevations. Attached to the northeast corner of the Main Block, the Library Wing (hereinafter "Library Wing") was constructed in two stages with its western half having been built in 1896 and its eastern half added in 1924 from designs produced by Bigelow & Wadsworth, architects. At the south end of the Main Block is a two-storey Kitchen Wing with a flat roof (hereinafter "Kitchen Wing") that was built by George Macomber Co. in 1949

PURPOSE 1. Purpose. It is the Purpose of this Restriction to assure that the architectural, historic, and cultural features of the Property and Building as defined in this document will be retained and maintained for the term of this Agreement substantially in their current condition for conservation and preservation purposes so that they continue to contribute to the historical setting and significance of the Harvard Square National Register Historic District; and any use or change of the Property or Building that will significantly impair or interfere with the Property's preservation values will be prohibited. GRANTOR'S COVENANTS 2.1 Grantor's Covenants: Covenant to Maintain. Grantor agrees at all times to maintain the Building in the same structural condition and state of repair as that existing on the effective date of this Restriction. Grantor's obligation to maintain shall require replacement, repair, and reconstruction by Grantor whenever necessary to preserve the Building in substantially the same structural condition and state of repair as that existing on the date of this Restriction. Grantor's obligation to maintain shall also require that the Property's landscaping be maintained in good appearance. Existing iron fences, modern wood fences and stone curbings shall be maintained in their present configurations; lawn areas and planting beds shall be maintained as either lawns or planting beds. Vegetation and screening shall be maintained such that the Building remains visible from the public rights of way on Mount Auburn Street and Holyoke Place. Subject to the casualty provisions of paragraphs 7 and 8, this obligation to maintain shall require replacement, rebuilding, repair, and reconstruction of the Building whenever necessary in accordance with The Secretary of the Interior's Standards for the Treatment of Historical Properties with Guidelines for Preserving, Rehabilitating, Restoring and Reconstructing Historic 4

GRANTOR'S CONDITIONAL RIGHTS 3.1 Conditional Rights Requiring Approval by Grantee. Without the prior express written approval of the Grantee, which approval may be withheld or conditioned in the sole discretion of Grantee, Grantor shall not undertake any of the following actions: (a) erect any external signs or external advertisements except: (i) such plaque as is permitted under paragraph 19 of this Restriction; (i) a sign stating solely the address of the Property; and (iii) a sign on the porch providing the name of the club and/ or identitying the Building as private; (b) make permanent substantial topographical changes, such as, by example, excavation for the construction of either above or below-ground parking areas, underground storage areas, and recreational facilities; (c) install condenser units and other exterior machinery necessary to the operation of central air conditioning, provided that such machinery shall be screened from view from the public right of way on Mount Auburn Street and Holyoke Place; and (d) undertake any activities to maintain the Building or the Property which may be considered to be of a major nature. INTERPRETATION 24. Interpretation. The following provisions shall govern the effectiveness, interpretation, and duration of the Restriction. Include uses of the buikding, and the role(s) th uners/occupants played within the communiț The Fly Club stands in the "Gold Coast" section of Harvard Square adjacent to numerous other finals clubs that were built in this area during the early twentieth century. The building's red-brick Colonial Revival design and domestic scale are characteristic of the club houses built for these organizations and follow closely the architectural style that became strongly associated with Harvard University under the presidency of Abbott Lawrence Lowell. The building was designed in 1896-1899 and modified in 1902 from plans be Herbert Dudley Hale (1866-1908), a Boston architect and son of the Reverend Everett Edward Hale. The Philadelphia Architects and Buildings website provides the following brief biography of Hale: Although chiefly known as a Boston architect, Herbert D. Hale and his partner Henry G. Morse maintained an office in Philadelphia from 1900 through 1905. Hale was born in Dorchester, MA, the son of Edward Everett Hale. He graduated from Marvard University in 1888 and attended the Ecole des Beaux Arts in Paris for four years. He worked in New York City for the years 1897-1898, and around the turn of the century established a firm in Boston with James Gamble Rogers, under the name of Hale & Rogers. He also maintained an association with New York architect Henry G. Morse, however; and it was under this firm that the work of Hale & Morse was executed. The main office of Hale & Morse was in New York City and the firm enjoyed a national practice with commissions in New Orleans (United States Post Office), Memphis, Tennessee (The Shelby County Courthouse) and Boston (South Boston High School). In addition to its Philadelphia office, the firm maintained offices in Chicago and Boston. Founded in 1836 as the Alpha Delta Phi Club, the Fly Club was given its present name in the mid-twentieth century "by combining the 'PH from Alpha, the T from Delta and the i from Phi, to get ; Phli" which was shortened to "Fly". Over the course of its history, the Club and this building have been associated with many prominent members including Franklin Delano Roosevelt, President of the United States; Abbott Lawrence Lowell, President of Harvard; and James Bryant Conant, President of Harvard. EXHIBIT F RESTRICTION GUIDELINES The purpose of these Preservation Guidelines is to clarify the terms of this Historic Preservation Restriction that deal with maintenance and alteration to the Building. Under Paragraph 3, prior permission from the Preservation Massachusetts, Incorporated (PMI) is required for any Minor Maintenance that is reasonably expected to materially change the appearance, materials, colors or workmanship from that existing prior to the maintenance and for any Major Maintenance. Minor Maintenance that is part of ordinary maintenance and repair and does not materially change the appearance, materials, colors or workmanship from that existing prior to the maintenance does not require PMI review and approval. In an effort to explain what constitutes Minor Maintenance and Major Maintenance, the following list is provided. The list is by no means comprehensive. It is a sampling of common structural alterations. PAINT Minor: Exterior or interior hand scraping and repainting of non-decorative and non-significant surfaces as part of periodic maintenance. Major: Painting or fully stripping decorative surfaces or distinctive stylistic features, including murals, stenciling, wallpaper, ornamental woodwork, stone, and decorative or significant original plaster. WINDOWS AND DOORS Minor: Regular maintenance including caulking, painting, and necessary reglazing. Repair or in-kind replacement of existing individual decayed window parts. Major: Wholesale replacement of units; change in fenestration or materials; alteration of profile or setback of windows. The addition of storm windows is also considered a major change; however, with notification it is commonly acceptable. EXTERIOR Minor: Spot repair of existing cladding and roofing including in-kind replacement of clapboard, shingles, slates, etc. Major: Large scale repair or replacement of cladding or roofing. Change involving inappropriate removal or addition of materials or building elements (i.e., removal of chimneys or cornice detailing; installation of architectural detail which does not have a historical basis); altering or demolishing building 48

LANDSCAPING/OUTBUILDINGS Minor: Routine maintenance of outbuildings and landscape including lawn mowing, pruning, planting, painting, and repair. HEATING/AIR CONDITIONING/ELECTRICAL/PLUMBING SYSTEMS Minor: Repair of existing systems. Major: Installing or upgrading systems which will result in major appearance changes to the exterior and interior (i.e. exterior mechanical equipment, dropped ceilings, disfigured walls or floors, exposed wiring, ducts and piping); the removal of substantial quantities of original plaster or other materials in the course of construction. Changes classified as Major Maintenance are not necessarily unacceptable. Under the Historic Preservation Restriction such changes must be reviewed by the PMI in order to assess their impact on the historic integrity of the Property, Building, and other structures. It is the responsibility of the Grantor to notify PMI in writing when any Minor Maintenance that is reasonably expected to materially change the appearance, materials, colors or workmanship from that existing prior to the maintenance or any Major Maintenance is contemplated. Substantial alterations may necessitate review of plans and specifications. The intent of this Historic Preservation Restriction is to enable the PMI to review proposed alterations and assess their impact on the integrity of the Property, the Building, and other structures, not to preclude future change. PMI staff will attempt to work with the Grantor to develop mutually satisfactory solutions which are in the best interests of the Property. 49
