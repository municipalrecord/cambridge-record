"Smoking" is defined at §8.28.050 as: "Inhaling, exhaling, burning or carrying any lighted cigarette, cigar, pipe tobacco, nicotine delivery product, snuff or tobacco in any manner or in any form." That statute prohibits marijuana and tobacco smoking in workplaces, restaurants, public buildings, and many other places. It also provides for local enforcement. If the City Council would like marijuana smoking regulated or restricted differently than what has been established under G.L. c. 270, §22, the Law Department could provide a draft ordinance for the Council's consideration based on policy decisions made by the Council. Similarly, if the City Council would like to restrict indoor marijuana smoking even more strictly than is currently done by G.L.c.270, §22, the Law Department can draft an ordinance that would include such provisions. Because state law has now been amended to address the regulation of some of the activities inquired about by the City Council in this Council Order, it would be helpful to have guidance from the City Council as to what additional provisions the Council would like to have regulated by the Cambridge City Code. II. Response to Council Order No. 0-2 of 1/29/18 Council Order No. 0-2 of 1/29/18 makes a related request, for a report on work underway regarding regulating the adult use of marijuana. In response to Council Order No. 0-2 of 1/29/18 concerning other aspects of regulating the adult use of marijuana, there are many issues for the Council to consider. As an initial caveat, however, it is important to note that the state's Cannabis Control Commission ("the CCC") has issued very complex and lengthy draft regulations (to be promulgated at 935 CMR 500.000) to implement the new adult use of marijuana statutes. Those regulations have been the subject of a multitude of public comments and questions, and it is probable that the draft regulations will be revised before they are finalized. Therefore, it would probably be a good idea for the Council not to adopt any local ordinance provisions until the state draft regulations regarding the adult use of marijuana have been finalized. Certain deadlines for state action on the adult use of marijuana are set forth in St.2017, c.55, §55. That law requires the CCC to: "promulgate regulations, guidelines and protocols necessary for the issuance of licenses pursuant to chapter 94G of the General Laws2 not later than March 15, 2018. Thus, March 15, 2018 is the deadline for the CCC to finalize its draft regulations. The next statutory deadline requires the CCC "to begin to accept applications for licenses pursuant to said chapter 94G not later than April 1, 2018. The CCC "shall not issue a license pursuant to chapter 94G of the General Laws until June 1,2018." St2017,c.55,§56(c). There are many aspects of the new laws and draft regulations that allow for, and in some cases require, local action in connection with the adult use of marijuana. These issues are complex, and the state regulations that will govern them are not finalized, but following is a list of some of the issues the City is expected to have to address: a. Zoning. There is currently no zoning ordinance provision that specifically governs the cultivation, manufacture, retail sale, or on-site usage of marijuana that is not medically prescribed. Zoning Ordinance section 11 .800 applies to medical marijuana only, requiring applicants for dispensaries to obtain a special permit from the Planning Board based on a number of considerations. b. Taxation. One of the many state laws that was amended as part of the adult use of marijuana legislation was G.L.C.64N, §3, which as a local option, allows a city or town that accepts that law to: ...impose a local sales tax upon sale or transfer of marijuana or marijuana products by a marijuana retailer operating within the city or town to anyone other than a marijuana establishment at a rate not greater than 3 per cent of the total sales price received by the marijuana retailer as a consideration for the sale of marijuana or marijuana products. No action is required by the City if the City does not want to tax marijuana sales. c. Host Community Agreements. The new law requires that every new adult use of marijuana establishment and every existing medical marijuana dispensary that wants to expand to have an adult use business must enter into a host community agreement with the municipality in which they are located. G.L.c.940, §3(d). Pursuant to that statute, a host community agreement "may include a community impact fee for the host community" as long as the fee is reasonably related to documented costs imposed on the community by the operation of the marijuana establishment, is no more than 3% of the gross sales of the marijuana establishment and is payable for no more than 5 years. submitted by the City Manager to the CCC on February 14,2018. Role of Municipalities The Marijuana Acts both authorize and limit the way in which municipalities can control marijuana establishments in their communities. It also protects any restrictions or limitations a municipality may have imposed as of July 1, 2017 on the operation of medical marijuana treatment centers, marijuana establishments or both, pursuant to the 2012 law authorizing medical use of marijuana (Chapter 369 of the Acts of 2012) or the 2016 Act. Below is a brief overview of provisions relating to municipal control. Any decision to implement local controls on marijuana should be made in consultation with a municipality's attorney. Bylaws & Ordinances The law allows, but does not require, municipalities to pass bylaws and ordinances governing the "time, place, and manner"' of marijuana establishments (cultivators, retailers, manufacturers, testing labs, and any other licensed marijuana-related businesses) as well as businesses dealing with marijuana accessories. Such bylaws and ordinances may not be "unreasonably impracticable." The agreement must stipulate the responsibilities of the community and the marijuana establishment or medical marijuana treatment centers. The agreement may include a community impact fee of up to 3% of gross sales to be paid to the host community, as long as the fee is reasonably related to real costs imposed on the municipality due to the establishment or medical marijuana treatment center operating there. The agreement may not be effective for longer than five years.
