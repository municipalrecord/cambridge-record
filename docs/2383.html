Attachment B Opening Remarks For July 11, 2016 Housing Committee Meeting Sullivan Chamber at 6 pm The Housing Committee will conduct a public hearing to continue the discussion regarding the recently completed Inclusionary Housing Study and the Affordable Housing Trust's recommendations to the City Council. This hearing to be televised. Good Evening, Tonight, the Housing Committee is holding the third of at least three meetings to discuss the recently-completed Inclusionary Zoning Study, which was undertaken by David Paul Rosen & Associates at the City's behest. The full 101-page report from this consultant is available on the City's website, and tonight's meeting will also focus on the recommendations from the Affordable Housing Trust that were drafted in response to this study. I am going to ask the members of the Trust to briefly walk us through their recommendations, then I will call for Public Comment, and finally, I will open the floor to my colleagues for questions, comments, and other thoughts they may have. As I have stated in the past, we must continually be cognizant of WHY this study was commissioned: in short, Cambridge is facing an extraordinary shortage of affordable housing. The reasons for the rising rental costs and the scarcity of truly affordable housing are not unique to Cambridge, and we have seen similar issues in Boston, in New York, in San Francisco, and in many, many other cities throughout the country. The Community Development Department has looked at how other communities have sought to deal with this issue, we have reached out to housing experts to study the options available to us, and they have worked exhaustively to arrive at the recommendations that represent our strongest chance at maximizing the amount of affordable housing we can build. These recommendations are based on an exhaustive, deliberate analysis of the conditions that

I know some people in the community have voiced concerns that this process is moving much too fast, and I just wanted to take a moment to provide some context and some assurances: last term, the Housing Committee spent the majority of our time focusing on the best way to raise the Linkage Fee rates. That discussion went through an exhaustive vetting process by the CDD, then by the Housing Committee, then by the Ordinance Committee, and then, finally, by the City Council. There were many, many opportunities for the public to weigh in on this, and we ended up with a truly solid piece of legislation that will yield great benefits for the City. When Linkage passed in 2015, I announced that the next item we would be looking at would be the Inclusionary Zoning ordinance. This is something that has been thoroughly investigated by our consultant, the consultant's report has been vetted by the Affordable Housing Trust, the Housing Committee is now holding its third hearing on this matter - and we still have months of Ordinance hearings and City Council discussions focusing on this in front of us. The next Housing Committee hearing to discuss this matter will be on Monday, August 15 at 5:30 pm in this room, so please circle that on your calendars and please know that I fully intend for this to be vetted as fully and as thoroughly as possible. But I DO want to keep this process moving forward. I also want to note that this meeting is being recorded and televised. I will now turn the floor over to my Co-Chair, Vice Mayor McGovern, to see if he has any opening thoughts, then I will cede the floor to the Affordable Housing Trust to walk us through their recommendations.
