The Committee had some questions for me at that meeting, which I answer below, and I suggest how the Committee can help with responding to the Council Order. Application of Anti-Aid Amendment to Community Benefit Funds When we met, we discussed whether the use of Community Benefit Funds is subject to the Anti-Aid Amendment of the Massachusetts Constitution. In a 1968 Opinion, the Supreme Judicial Court (SJC) issued an opinion answering questions of the Massachusetts Senate, and in that opinion determined that "Public money' is money raised by taxation." Opinion of the Justs., 354 Mass. 779, 784 (1968) (citing Opinion of the Justices, 337 Mass. 777, 781 (1958)). Therefore, the SJC determined that the Anti-Aid Amendment did not apply to funds that are not raised by taxation. Id. Community Benefits Funds as defined in the Ordinance are "funds offered to and received by the City in connection with the enactment of an amendment to the City's Zoning Ordinance or other agreements entered into with the City which provide funds to the City and are held by the City to be expended for community benefits or other related purposes." Accordingly, the funds are not raised by taxation, and a court would likely find are not public money subject to the Anti-Aid Amendment. Even if the funds were subject to the Anti-Aid Amendment, it does not bar the grant of funds to a private entity but requires a fact specific analysis of whether the use of the funds is permissible. In Commonwealth v. School Comm. of Springfield, 382 Mass. 665 (1981), the Supreme Judicial Court established a three-part test to assess whether expenditure of public funds violates the Anti-Aid Amendment: 1) what is the purpose of the spending because it has to be for a public purpose; 2) to what extent the spending aids a private entity because substantial assistance is likely not permissible; and 3) whether the spending avoids "the political and economic abuses which prompted the passage of [the Anti-Aid Amendment]." A common finding for grants that do not violate the third part of the test is that the benefits were available to the general public and not selectively beneficial to certain organizations. The Springfield test is "not precise" and serves instead as "guidelines to a proper analysis." Caplan v. Town of Acton, 479 Mass. 69, 74 (2018). The application of the Springfield test is fact-specific and focuses on the particular expenditures at issue. The Use of Community Benefits Funds for Capital Expenses Currently the Ordinance prohibits uses funds for "capital or infrastructure projects or general operational expenses." If the City Council wants to amend the Ordinance to remove that prohibition, Community Benefits Funds could then be given to organizations for capital or operational expenses. When there are funds that are subject to the Anti-Aid Amendment, the use of the funds for capital or operating expenses may be in violation of the Anti-Amendment. It will be necessary to do a fact specific analysis to determine if it is a public purpose to fund capital or operating expenses, whether the contribution to the capital or operating expenses is substantial assistance for the organization, and whether the offer of funds was widely available or only available to one or a small group of recipients. If the funds are not subject to the Anti-Aid Amendment, which appears to be the case with Community Benefit Funds, there is legally no restrictions on how the funds are used, unless the funds are granted to the City for a specific purpose. However, even without any legal restrictions in place, the City has a strong interest in having funds go to uses that benefit the community. Assistance Needed from the Committee Awaiting Report 25-40 directs the City Manager and City Solicitor to consult with the Committee about amendments to the Ordinance "that allow for the use of funds for capital expenses, provided that such expenses clearly advance the goals of the Community Benefits program." Therefore, it would be helpful to hear from the Committee what recommendations it has for parameters around how funds can be used for capital expenses that still advance the goals of the Community Benefits program. 2
