A communication was received from Paula M. Crane, Deputy City Clerk, transmitting a report from Councillor Craig Kelley, Chair of the Public Safety Committee and Mayor E. Denise Simmons and Vice Mayor Marc C. McGovern, Co-Chairs of the Housing Committee, for a joint public hearing held on July 19, 2016 to discuss the presence and impact of short-term rental units (Airbnb, FlipKey, VRBO, etc.) in Cambridge, and to hear suggestions from community members and operators on how best to address the challenges of this emerging market.

CAMBRIDGE CITY COUNCIL Craig A. Kelley City Councilor TO: Donna Lopez, City Clerk FROM: Craig A. Kelley, City Councillor July 18, 2016 DATE: SUBJECT: Questions submitted to short-term rental service providers Airbnb, Inc., and FlipKey, Inc., for discussion at the joint hearing of the Public Safety and Housing Committees, 19,2016 In the weeks leading up to the first joint Public Safety and Housing Committee's meeting on shoti-term rentals and #SharingCambridge, our office engaged with several neighborhood and business communities along with interested constituents to understand what questions they would ask of this growing market. In the midst of that process, a representative of Airbnb invited our office to submit a list of questions in advance of the July 19 meeting for their staff to answer. We saw the invitation as an opportunity for the public to ask questions directly to short-term rental services, and have included their queries with our own. Our office also submitted a copy of the questions below to a representative ofFlipKey, headquatiered in Boston, for their response as well. While not exhaustive, we hope these questions will help frame a better conversation which highlights the unique character and circumstances of Cambridge, how multi-national corporations access our communities, and where opportunities for dialogue exist. The following questions were forwarded to Will Bums of Airbnb, Inc., and Kari Jorgenson ofFlipKey, Inc., on Friday, July 15, 2016. 1. How does your company plan to address the findings of a recent Harvard University study that found "widespread discrimination" on short-term rental sites like Airbnb? (Edelman, Luca, Svirsky, "Racial Discrimination in the Sharing Economy: Evidence from a Field Experiment," (Jan. 6, 2016)). 2. How many listings does your company have in Cambridge, operators have more than one listed property in Cambridge, and what percentage do they represent? 3. What is your company's position on policies of limiting shm1-term rentals to primary residents (owner-occupied) only? 4. What is your company's position on a regulation requiring all operators in Cambridge obtain and post a license? 5. What are the best regulatory practices used by cities that resemble Cambridge in population, housing stock, and housing prices?
