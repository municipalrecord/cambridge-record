December 17, 2023 Cambridge City Council via email Subject: Please Pass the Proposed BEUDO Amendment for New Buildings Dear City Councillors: Green Cambridge appreciates the City Council’s extraordinary achievement in passing the June 26th amendments to the Building Energy Use Disclosure Ordinance (BEUDO), setting net zero requirements for large existing buildings. We also thank you for adopting the ordinance that would allow Cambridge to participate in the 10-Town Fossil-Fuel-Free Demonstration Program for new construction. We urge the Council to complete this important work by approving this BEUDO amendment to close the gap that exists between these two programs for laboratory, medical and research buildings. By requiring newly constructed BEUDO-eligible buildings to reach net zero on an accelerated timeline, by 2030, these amendments would incentivize otherwise exempt new lab, medical and research new buildings to be built without fossil fuel infrastructure. Commercial and institutional buildings are the largest source of greenhouse gas emissions in Cambridge (https://www.cambridgema.gov/CDD/climateandenergy/greenhousegasemissions/ ). Laboratory space makes up about 57% of the total commercial/institutional square footage in Cambridge, but approximately 70% of the City’s total commercial/institutional greenhouse gas emissions, because of laboratories’ intensive energy use. (BEUDO 2021 Map | Open Data Portal | City of Cambridge (cambridgema.gov) It is the high ventilation rates that make laboratory and medical buildings such huge energy users. Having their energy use be as renewable as possible right from the start, when they are new, is critically important to Cambridge’s greenhouse gas reduction goals. We learned at the November 28th Ordinance Committee meeting that laboratory designers can reasonably use air source heat pumps (mini-splits) to meet all laboratory building heating and cooling needs for all but the coldest days, when the outdoor temperature is eight degrees Fahrenheit or lower. For a substantial increase in cost, additional heat pumps could be added to provide the needed heat on these days, but since these coldest days occur only two percent of the time, this is not considered cost-effective. The most cost-effective approach is to have a fossil-fuel-fired “peaking boiler” or heater that would provide supplemental heat on these coldest

The laboratory designers reportedly stated that this is the approach that they would take to comply with the new state Specialized Energy Stretch Code, that went into effect in Cambridge on July 1, 2023. The Specialized Energy Stretch Code for Commercial Construction allows for any of three pathways for compliance: 1) All Electric Building Performance Standard. 2) Mixed-Fuel Building Performance Standard. This allows use of fossil-fuel-fired space heating, hot water and appliances, as long as the building has “pre-wiring and electrical service provision to the building to allow for future electrification” and also has minimum specified solar panels. 3) Zero Energy Building Performance Standard. This requires net zero energy on an annual basis from the first year of construction, based on on-site renewable energy generation. Presumably, the laboratory designers who met with CDD would be using Option 2). This option does NOT limit new laboratories to fossil-fuel-fired heat for the coldest days only. Fossil fuel equipment could be used for primary space heating, as long as the building is wired for future electric conversion, and it is on a conversion path “that is consistent with a net-zero Massachusetts economy in 2050.” (https://www.mass.gov/doc/summary-document-explaining- stretch-energy-code-and-specialized-opt-in-code-language/ ) The amendment language proposed by Councillor Zondervan at the November 28th Ordinance Committee meeting is an excellent compromise. By exempting laboratory and medical facility fossil fuel use on the “coldest days” (8 deg. F or colder), and allowing smaller facilities to use carbon offset credits, the City would be granting flexibility and the ability for new laboratories to take a cost-effective approach to be as reasonably all-electric as they currently can. By requiring that new laboratories and medical facilities achieve net zero by 2030, the BEUDO amendments would provide an incentive to do so. Otherwise, laboratories could use the Specialized Energy Stretch Code Mixed-Fuel Performance Standard to avoid electrification that they say they can do now. Green Cambridge believes the time is right to encourage new builders of our most energy- intensive and greatest greenhouse-gas-emitting land use to do it correctly now! Green Cambridge urges the City Council to adopt the BEUDO amendments with Councillor Zondervan’s November 28th changes. Sincerely,
