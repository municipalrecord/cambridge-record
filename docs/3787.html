Attachment C Health Department Response to the Opioid Crisis (2016-2017) June 14, 2017 The Cambridge Public Health Department (CHD) works closely with city and community partners to raise awareness about the disease of addiction and to develop strategies for preventing opioid misuse and abuse, as well as preventing opioid-related overdoses and deaths. City and community partners have taken a strategic approach to addressing the impact of the statewide opioid crisis on Cambridge. These efforts are focused on both "upstream" prevention- such as encouraging residents to safely store and dispose of prescription pain medications--to saving lives by expanding the availability of naloxone, a drug that reverses the effects of a heroin overdose. Many organizations and groups are involved in the local opioid crisis response, including: • AIDS Action Committee (Needle Exchange and Overdose Prevention Program) • Cambridge Department of Human Service Programs • Cambridge Fire Department • Cambridge Health Alliance • Cambridge Police Department • Cambridge Public Health Department - OPEN (Overdose Prevention & Education Network) - Cambridge Prevention Coalition • Cambridge-Somerville Healthcare for the Homeless • CASPAR, Inc. • Eliot Community Human Services • Emergency Medical Services (Pro EMS, Cataldo EMS) • Institute for Health and Recovery • Learn to Cope • North Charles, Inc. City and community partners offer a comprehensive range of services across the continuum of substance abuse care-prevention, intervention, treatment, and recovery support. Please note: The following section summarizes initiatives with which the Cambridge Public Health Department is directly involved, and does not capture the programs and activities of city and community partners. Coordination, Coalition-Building and Data Collection 2015 City of Cambridge Community Health Improvement Plan The Cambridge Public Health Department is facilitating the implementation of city's first community health improvement plan (CHIP), which is setting the city's health agenda through 2020. Mental and Behavioral Health and Substance Abuse is one of the four CHIP priority areas. The CHIP outlines specific objectives for addressing opioid prevention and treatment over the next five years. communities of Everett, Somerville, and Watertown. As the lead, CPHD coordinates regional and Cambridge-specific activities to raise awareness about prescription opioid safety and prevent overdoses. OPEN is funded by a federal grant awarded by the Massachusetts Department of Public Health. Data Collection The Cambridge Public Health Department has developed a local opioid overdose surveillance system. In 2016, staff designed a system for collecting and analyzing real-time data from Pro EMS monthly data from Cambridge Health Alliance, and annual data from AIDS Action Committee' Needle Exchange and Overdose Prevention Program. CPHD will begin generating formal data summary reports in late 2017. Prevention Opioid Crisis News & Events OPEN shared timely information about opioid prevention, intervention, and treatment at community events and through its website, Twitter account, and e-newsletter. Community-Level Prevention Strategy Session OPEN organized and hosted the second annual Opioid Community Summit in May 2016 in which stakeholders from Cambridge, Everett, Somerville, and Watertown developed community-level strategies for prevention for the coming fiscal year. School Community: • In fall 2016, CPHD began implementing a five-year, $425,000 Partnership for Success federal grant awarded to Cambridge by the Massachusetts Department of Public Health to reduce prescription drug misuse among the city's public high school students. CPHD staff have attended multiple skills trainings to learn about the federal Strategic Prevention Framework model and facilitating social change; conducted stakeholder interviews with a parent and provider, and organized a youth focus group in April 2017 in which 20 CRLS student spoke about drug and alcohol use in the community. • In early 2017, trained staff from the Cambridge Public Health Department and Teen Health Center individually interviewed 430 ninth graders to assess their risk for substance abuse using the evidenced-based "Screening, Brief Intervention and Referral for Treatment (SBIRT)" approach. • In May 2017, the Cambridge Public Health Department and CRLS hosted an "Evening with Chris Herren," a former rising NBA star, who spoke to students and families about his struggles with addiction and how students can stand up against substance abuse. Over 100 people attended the event. Medication Disposal • The Cambridge Police Department, Cambridge Public Health Department (primarily through OPEN), and other city departments continue to encourage residents to safely dispose of unwanted medications by sponsoring community "take-back" events; accepting medication at the city's Department of Public Works' four household hazardous waste collection days; offering prepaid mailers to the public; and maintaining a 24/7 medication disposal kiosk at the police department. In 2016, the city collected over 9,800 pounds of unwanted medication. In 2016-2017, medication take-back events were held in April 2016 (police dept.), Sept. 2016 (senior center); April 2017 (police dept.), May 2017 (Chris Herren event at CRLS), and at the five DPW household hazardous waste collection days to date. 2

• OPEN produced a 30-second public service announcement on safe medication disposal, which aired on CCTV during fiscal year 2017. intervention Business Community: Overdose Response Training OPEN is collaborating with and the Cambridge Needle Exchange and Overdose Prevention Program (part of AIDS Action Committee) to offer overdose recognition and response training to Cambridge business owners. We are offering training opportunities to interested businesses with public bathrooms. Bystander Intervention • OPEN produced advertisements about the state's Good Samaritan Law that were displayed throughout April 2017 on 400 interior placards on MBTA buses in Cambridge and 20+ other communities, making over an estimated 3 million impressions. Poster and flyer versions of the ads were disseminated to partners and posted around Cambridge. The Good Samaritan Law protects victims and people who call 911 for help from charge, prosecution, and conviction for possession or use of controlled substances. • The PARK Study (Public Access opioid Reversal Kit), conducted by researchers at Brigham & Women's Hospital in partnership with the Cambridge Police Department, aims to evaluate the feasibility of laypeople using a public access opioid reversal kit to respond to an overdose. In April 2017, the PARK Study team tested the protocol and gathered data on user experience with a proof-of-concept study in Lafayette Park. CPHD staff participated in the study. Upcoming Events & Activities • OPEN is hosting the third annual Opioid Community Summit at Somerville Hospital on Wednesday, June 21 at 6:30 p.m. • OPEN is creating a series "roll call" videos about addiction. These short videos will be shown . during roll call at fire and police stations across the region, and will touch on topics such as addiction, stigma, and interacting with drug users. • The Partnership for Success program will recruit high school-aged teens to help create a social marketing campaign on the non-medical use of prescription drugs. 3
