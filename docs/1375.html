TESTIMONY OF Trista Adams CAMBRIDGE ORDINANCE 6.18 Good afternoon, my name is Trista Adams and I am here on behalf of Feld Entertainment, the producer of Ringling Bros. and Barnum & Bailey, Disney on Ice and other live family entertainment We are strongly opposed to proposed Ordinance 6.18, which would prohibit traveling exhibitors and circuses with wild and exotic animals from walking, exhibiting or operating in the City of Cambridge. Ringling Bros. is the largest and most well-known traveling exhibitor of animals in the world, with over 145 years of experience working with and caring lor wild and exotic animals. Our shows travel throughout the U.S. and perform annually in over 125 cities, including Boston. While we do not have performances within the City of Cambridge, each year the train that carries most of the people, equipment and some of the animals for our shows unloads and parks within Cambridge while our show is in the City of Boston. For approximately one week we utilize the state-owned train yard as the base of operation for our Boston circus engagement We plan to continue to use the train yard even when we no longer have elephants in our traveling shows. It appears that this ordinance could prevent Ringling Bros. from continuing to do so and we believe that for that and other reasons it is legally suspect. We believe that this ordinance is constitutionally flawed in at least two respects: First, this ban unconstitutionally infringes on Freedom of Speech by prohibiting licensed exhibitors from conducting their performance with animals, just because some portion of society prefers a different form of entertainment This is nothing less than content based censorship. There are a multitude of studies that have found that the act of traveling or performing is not adverse to the welfare of animals in traveling exhibitions. As I mentioned, Ringling Bros. has more than 145 years of experience working with and caring ~ for wild and exotic animals. We have a full time veterinary staff that includes some of the leading experts on elephants and other exotic animals. We adhere to stringent internal animal- care guidelines and the best practices for feeding, safety, training, health, housing and transport. we ensure compliance with all federal, state and local laws and regulations. At the federal level, all circuses and other traveling exhibitors with animals are required to be licensed by the United States Department of Agriculture (USDA). USDA frequently inspects licensed exhibitors, including Ringling Bros., to ensure they at all times comply with the requirements ot the Animal welfare Act. This is the same law that governs zoos and sanctuaries. Additionally, Ringling Bros. and other circuses are subject to all state and local laws and regulations. All traveling exhibitors are required to obtain permits from the Department of Fish and Game before entering Massachusetts. While performing in Boston, we are often inspected by the Massachusetts Society for the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals and have never been cited or charged for anything related to our animal care. We have never opposed the adoption of reasonable and effective regulatory measures tor purposes of ensuring the welfare of all animals. However, an outright ban on traveling shows or circus that includes wild or exotic animals within the City of Cambridge is unnecessarily and unreasonably restrictive and would prevent lawful, state and federally regulated exhibitors from being able to conduct business. /'Many of the same animal rights organizations that advocate for these bans have attacked our company in the courts and have consistently lost. Most notably, several organizations, including the Humane Society of the United States, the American Society for the Prevention of Cruelty to Animals, Born Free and the Animal Welfare Institute, paid Feld Entertainment $25 million to settle such litigation in 2014. We believe this speaks to the lack of creditability of the . types of organizations that push these measures.
