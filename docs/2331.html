Memorandum To: Mayor E. Denise Simmons, Vice Mayor Marc McGovern & Council Member Craig Kelley Cc: Paula Crane & Donna Lopez; Committee Staff From: Matt Mincieli, Northeast Region Executive Director, TechNet Date: July 18, 2016 Re: Testimony re Public Safety and Housing Committees joint public hearing to discuss the operation and prevalence of short-term rental units. Dear Chair Simmons, Chair McGovern and Chair Kelley, and through you to the members of the Housing and Public Safety Committees, my name is Matt Mincieli and I’m the Northeast Region executive director for TechNet. TechNet is a state and federal policy trade association made up of more than 70 of the nation’s leading technology and innovation companies. TechNet (www.technet.org) is committed to advancing the public policies and private sector initiatives that make the U.S. the most innovative country in the world. TechNet’s partnership with policy makers paves the way for technology companies to create, compete and thrive. On behalf of our membership, we respectfully submit this letter to ask that the Committees support reasonable regulations that protect the public, but allow this segment of the innovative industry and sharing economy to grow in Cambridge. As with all innovative sharing economy platforms, there is a certain amount of disruption as new economies compete with existing economies. This disruption demands that policy makers be thoughtful, flexible and creative when considering new regulations and avoid the temptation to force new technology in to existing regulatory frameworks. Many other municipalities across the country have already successfully embraced and regulated the sharing economy in a way that allows it to grow and flourish to the benefit of residents and tourists alike. The essence of home sharing is to maximize a person’s economic potential, while at the same time providing a much-needed service that provides economic benefits to the sharer and the consumer previously unrealized until the advent of new technology. Research shows the overwhelming majority of people who use a home sharing platform to rent out their property only own one home, and a large majority need the money to supplement their income from another job. Home sharing also allows business travelers and tourists another option when deciding on where to travel and where to stay, as traditional lodging options continue to increase in price, adding increasing stress on travel budgets.

As with any new industry, there will be bad actors, but the home sharing industry has already made huge strides in self-policing and preventing those bad actors from taking advantage of the technology by listing multiple properties. Also, many municipalities have laid down a road map for how to put in to place regulatory safeguards, without impeding new innovation or the existing lodging industry, that would further enhance Cambridge’s reputation for embracing new technology. In closing, we thank you for your attention to TechNet’s letter and again ask the Committees to consider in your deliberations the many economic benefits associated with home sharing and its impact on the local innovation industry that is creating a new economy in Cambridge. If TechNet or our member companies can be of any assistance to your Committees as you deliberate this important subject, please feel free to consider us a resource and contact me at [email removed].
