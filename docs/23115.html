City Hall Annex • 344 Broadway • Cambridge • Massachusetts • 02139 [phone removed] • www.cambridgema.gov Julie Wormser | Chief Climate Officer To: Yi-An Huang, City Manager From: Julie Wormser, Chief Climate Officer Date: December 5, 2025 Re: Cambridge Natural Hazard Mitigation Plan Final Adoption ____________________________________________________________________________ On January 28, 2025, FEMA issued an “Approval of the City of Cambridge Natural Hazard Mitigation Plan Pending local Adoption.” In order for the plan to be considered formally complete, allowing FEMA funds to flow to the City, the Council must now adopt the revised plan using required language provided by FEMA. We respectfully request the Council’s adoption of the plan. The HMP was initially adopted by Council in December 2023 and was subsequently submitted to the Massachusetts Emergency Management Agency (MEMA) and the Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) for review. Based on their review revisions were made, including on these key topics: - Improved description of how the community will continue to seek future public participation after the plan has been approved. - Refining narrative on Cambridge’s past changes in land use and projected changes in land use and how these changes affect risk and vulnerability to climate hazards - Specifying funding sources for mitigation actions - Adding state hazard declarations in addition to federal hazard declarations - Adding details related to high-hazard dams owned by Cambridge outside of the city The revisions resulted in the FEMA’s approval of the plan which can be accessed here. Background on the process to develop the HMP The HMP outlines Cambridge’s vulnerability to and impacts from natural hazards including climate change and describes what actions the City will take to prepare for and respond to natural hazards. The Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) requires an up-to-date HMP for municipalities to remain eligible for certain types of FEMA funding. The development of the HMP was guided by a cross-departmental steering committee, with consultant support from Weston & Sampson. The project team and steering committee worked to identify Cambridge’s vulnerability to natural hazards, consider the impact on the city’s people and assets, and create and prioritize mitigation actions aligned with the HMP goals, guided by public input.
