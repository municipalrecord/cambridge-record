Equity and Resilience in the NZAP

8 • The current NZAP identifies how actions that reduce GHG emissions also can improve equity and resiliency. • Examples of actions that can enhance equity and resilience include: • Programs designed to help support low-to-moderate income residents • Building retrofit programs that also include advising on resilience measures. • Annual reporting on the progress of the NZAP by OOS staff • The Plan requires oversight and annual review by the advisory

Climate Committee

• The NZAP is required to be updated every 5 years through a full review and community-based process. • The last update process was conducted in 2021, with Council adoption in 2023. 9 • The required five-year update takes into account many changes: • Outcomes of NZAP actions implemented to date • Evolution of technology (e.g., building electrification) • New state and federal policy and other regulatory developments • Changing costs and economics • Changing stakeholder needs • The update provides a framework for checking progress and adjusting our climate work, similar to the Commonwealth’s 5-year climate plan updates • OOS will begin the next five-year update in spring 2026 10 • The 2026 update will include: • Review changing conditions since last Plan update • Projection of emissions outcomes in 2030 and 2050 • The City will conduct an equitable community engagement process • Gather input on questions of energy affordability, scalability, and equity • This input and additional research will help develop updated actions, extending beyond 2030 11 Agenda • Overview of the Net

New Buildings

• As one of the first, Cambridge adopted the Specialized Stretch Code in 2023, requiring all new buildings to be highly energy efficient. • 57 Massachusetts municipalities have now adopted the SSC • Further, Cambridge is one of 10 Fossil Fuel Free communities in Massachusetts allowed to require nearly all new buildings, major renovations, and building additions to only use all-electric energy systems.

Existing Buildings

• Starting in January 2026 the BEUDO amendments require large, non-residential buildings to begin reducing emissions on a set schedule: • BEUDO emissions represent 50% of total GHG emissions in Cambridge Cambridge is implementing leading-edge climate action 13

Residential Buildings and Small Businesses

• The Electrify Cambridge program has provided no-cost advising on all-electric solutions to over 1,359 households, and now includes cool roofs support • The Green Equipment Program (ARPA funded) is on track to help 20 businesses decarbonize their operations through heat pumps, kitchen equipment, etc. • The number of Cambridge households getting home energy assessments, weatherization, and heat pumps is at a historic high. The City programs provide energy bill checkups and connect people to clean energy and efficiency programs. Cambridge is implementing leading-edge climate action 14
