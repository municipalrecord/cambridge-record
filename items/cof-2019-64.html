RE: City Council Order 0-17

October 7, 2019 Meeting

Dear City Manager DePasquale, This letter is in response to City Council Order 0-17 submitted at the October 7, 2019 Meeting which requested that an independent financial analysis be done of the proposed development pro forma by New England Development of the Galleria As part of this review I have had several meetings with Mall. representatives of New England Development to analyze the documentation in support of the pro forma of the proposed redevelopment of the Galleria Mall.

The impact of E-Commerce on traditional shopping malls has been dramatic with over 12,000 retail stores representing over 145 million square feet being closed during 2018 and 2019 on pace to exceed those numbers. Large well known retailers including Sears, Macy's, Best Buy, Toys R Us and J.C. Penney have ceased operation completely or closed large numbers of retail locations as a result of changing consumer tastes and preferences.

Traditional shopping malls were built based upon attracting "anchor" stores which occupied large spaces at the ends of the shopping centers with "in line" stores connecting the "anchor" The well known "anchor" stores were the attraction for stores. consumers to come to the shopping center which allowed for higher rents for the "in line" stores off setting lower rents for the "anchor" stores.

With the E-Commerce boom the large "anchor" stores are no long as viable and are being closed or scaled back in record numbers. As reported on CNBC it has been estimated by Credit Suisse that 25% of the remaining 1,100 malls in the US are and that 75% of the at risk of closure in the next five years mall revenues come from just 20% of US malls.

As a result a new retail experience needs to evolve to drive foot traffic with a mix of dining, office, entertainment, hotel and residential to replace the former "anchor" space which can create the necessary synergy of all the pieces working together harmoniously for the health and vibrancy of the redevelopment.

The Macy's building currently with 125, 000 square feet will become a 355,000 square foot building a net increase of 230,000 square feet. The Best Buy building currently with 113,000 square feet will become a 335,000 square foot building a net increase of 222,000 square feet. The Up Parking Garage with 213,000 square feet will become a 310,000 square foot building a net increase of 37,000 square feet.

The Sear's building currently with 124,000 square feet becoming a 210,000 square foot building a net increase of 86,000 square feet. The resulting redevelopment would result in 400,000 square feet in new commercial space and 175,000 square feet of residential space for a total of 575,000 square feet of new net space.

The redevelopment of The Galleria Mall will not be a simple process given that the mall will remain open for business during the renovation project making it more costly and time consuming than a new construction project. The process of keeping the mall open during construction causes a phasing of the project adding costs to the development which also extends the period of renovation increasing risk and construction costs.

Retrofitting the existing structures are more costly in a number of items to be able to support the additional height limit of the proposed structures including foundation premiums in the $40 million dollars to $50 million dollars. The current economic conditions are favorable to such a development but future economic uncertainty increases the risk of such a long term project with an expected eight to ten year build out.

Given the additional costs associated with the redevelopment the total projected construction cost will be approximately 88% of the estimated completion value. The mix of approximately 70% commercial and 30% residential is on the lower side of typical developments using a 608/40% split on new construction projects.

The high costs of construction including renovation associated with this development makes the 70% commercial and 30% residential reasonable especially given the commitment to 50% affordable housing units.

Any potential increase in affordable units would need to come at the expense of the market rate units in order to maintain a viable project.

The Planning Board is also requiring that the building constructed be the residential component of the development which while ensuring it be built also increases the risk.
