This approach has become the blueprint for addressing climate change through a lens of equity at the scale and pace called for by science, and our nation's young people have risen up through the Sunrise Movement to demand its implementation. Frustrating gridlock has prevented meaningful progress even under President Biden, however, and progressive cities like Cambridge cannot afford to wait for top-down solutions.

In 2021 I worked with Councillor Carlone, Sunrise Cambridge, and others to introduce the Green New Deal Zoning Petition.

This proposal offered one of the first municipal adaptations of the Green New Deal concept by establishing a mechanism for emissions accounting during the special permit process, charging new commercial developments for all of their emissions, and using the money to fund emissions reductions projects and green jobs training programs that would prepare unemployed or underemployed Cambridge residents and young people in particular for good-paying green careers.

This zoning petition was discussed by the Planning Board on June 29, 2021 and by the Ordinance Committee on September 21, 2021 before the previous council let it expire without taking action. The feedback we received during last year's process has substantially informed our resubmission of the proposal. The Planning Board told us that aspects of the proposal related to charging for emissions and promoting green jobs did not belong in zoning.

The Law Department cautioned us to avoid creating legal interdependencies within the municipal code. And CDD reminded us that future emissions reductions should be regulated through the Building Energy Use Ordinance rather than the special permit process. In response to this feedback and with support from several colleagues on the Council, I've retooled the proposal as a three-part package which accomplishes all of the original objectives in a way that is more compatible with our municipal code.

Part 1: Emissions Accounting Zoning Petition.

This petition amends Article 22 of the zoning code (Green Building Requirements) to clarify and standardize what must be provided through the existing emissions accounting requirement, including an embodied emissions calculation for new non-residential buildings.

Crucially, the current language doesn't define "total greenhouse gas emissions": (c) Net Zero Narrative: A written description of how the Green Building Project is being designed in response to the City's Net Zero Action Plan, which seeks to neutralize greenhouse gas emissions resulting from buildings by reducing their energy use intensity and promoting renewable sources of energy.

The Ragon Institute's Net Zero Narrative' closely follows the CDD template, making it relatively straightforward to determine the projected total emissions for this building. It's unclear from the Net Zero Narrative, however, whether tenant plug loads (energy expected to be consumed by the companies renting space in the building) are taken into account in these calculations; it seems unlikely based on the numbers. If not, the reported emissions cannot be considered "total" emissions for the proposed building.

Also the source energy use intensity (EUI) is left blank, and there is an erroneous calculation in the "Misc. Equipment" row showing a value of "6600%" (of total; the apparently correct value would be more like 18%). I'm not pointing this out to be nitpicky, but rather to show that the information is not being carefully scrutinized; if it were, this kind of obvious error would have been noticed and corrected during the Special Permit review process.

A Net Zero Narrative documenting anticipated energy use and emissions was not included in the application materials posted online for 180 Fawcett Street. At my request, CDD provided the Green Building Report, which includes a Net Zero Narrative, and has agreed to post it online. The report I received is quite detailed and does present several different building HVAC options, for example, but all four options presented produce the exact same emissions, which seems implausible.

The four tables detailing the different options appear to be nearly identical copies of each other. In this case tenant plug loads were clearly identified as 9% of total energy use in the building which seems reasonable. For The Garage project, some energy use is reported in Appendix C of their Green Building Report, but the required Net Zero Narrative is not included, and there are no total energy use or total emissions calculations provided.

Of the three reports, only the Ragon Institute (already approved) provided a clear and easy to read summary of the building's expected energy use and emissions. The current guidelines provided by CDD, when followed, yield only two snapshots of building emissions: one presumably anticipating the first year of building operation, and another anticipating some future year when the building has achieved net zero emissions.
