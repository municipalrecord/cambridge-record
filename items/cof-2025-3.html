CPP is an ambitious, newly-launched program that seeks to expand access to high- quality preschool programming for Cambridge families. The program has been designed to ensure that all young children in Cambridge, and especially those furthest from access to resources and opportunity, are set up for success in kindergarten and beyond. What are the goals of CPP? • Expand preschool access – To ensure that all Cambridge four- year-olds (and some three-year-olds) have access to high-quality early childhood education.

Through the system design, children from low-income families are prioritized for preschool seats. • Enhance preschool quality – To ensure that Cambridge preschool programs provide high-quality, evidence-based early childhood programming to children and families. • Connect and align the early childhood ecosystem – To support a high-quality and more aligned B-Grade 3 continuum. 5 What is a mixed-delivery system?

An approach to providing Universal Pre-Kindergarten (UPK) that distributes Pre-K seats across a variety of service- delivery settings. In systems across the country, preschool settings can include public schools, charter schools, community-based preschool / childcare programs, Head Start programs, etc. In Cambridge, the mixed-delivery system includes CPS classrooms, DHSP classrooms, and community- based programs. What are the requirements for programs to be included in CPP?

In order to be a CPP provider, preschool programs must: Provide full-day, school-year preschool (at least a 6.5-hour day). Provide high-quality preschool programming. Starting in 2015, a number of community providers participated in the Cambridge Scholarship Program, the precursor to CPP. Early 2020 – All Cambridge preschool programs were invited to an information session, and subsequently invited to indicate interest in becoming a CPP partner. All interested programs participated in quality / needs assessment.

Those that met the requirements above were included in CPP. How were CPP programs selected? Research-based, state-approved curriculum

Highly-qualified educators

Focus on social-emotional development and school readiness through play-based learning CPP is a publicly-funded program that provides free, school-day, school-year preschool within a mixed-delivery system, to all four-year-olds (and some three- year-olds) who are Cambridge residents. 6 The CPP mixed-delivery system includes a wide range of preschool program options. • CPP includes 23 community-based programs across 42 sites. (Some programs have multiple sites.) • Of our 23 community partners, four are considered to be “for profit”: • Two of those “for profit” entities are small family childcare centers. • One is a locally-based organization. • One is a national corporate provider.

Cambridge Public Schools

Department of Human Services Programs (DHSP)

Community-Based Programs

• Cambridge Public Schools currently offers CPP seats across 12 schools. • Program options include traditional preschool programs, Montessori programs, and language-immersion programs (Spanish, Portuguese, and Mandarin). • Preschool programming is also offered to children with special needs through a range of program options, including substantially separate classrooms and inclusion classrooms. • DHSP currently offers CPP seats across 7 sites.

(For SY25-26, DHSP will offer CPP seats across 9 sites.) 7 CPP is a family-choice-based system that enables each eligible family to select from a range of preschool options based on their individual family interests and needs. Who is eligible for CPP? • All four-year-olds who are Cambridge residents and who turn four by September 1 of each academic year. Four-year-olds from low-income families are prioritized for seats. • Some three-year-olds who turn three by September 1 of each academic year.

Three-year-olds are prioritized if they meet the following criteria: How do families receive CPP seats? • CPP is a matching system that uses a single, centralized online application to allow for a simplified and streamlined process for families.

(Rather than having to apply to multiple programs separately, families can apply to multiple programs through this single application.) • Families can select and rank up to 10 programs. • A matching algorithm matches families with available seats based on families’ selections and rankings. • Family is eligible for free/reduced lunch (for CPS). • Family income at or below 65% of area median income. • Family is currently experiencing homelessness. • Child is in foster care.

Additionally, a limited number of three-year-old seats are available for non-priority students in CPS and DHSP classrooms. 8 How did we get here?

9 CPP evolved from a longstanding commitment from city leaders to universal high- quality early childhood education, prioritizing children who have historically been furthest from access to high-quality preschool. • In 2015 the Cambridge Early Childhood Task Force issued its recommendations, which included a recommendation to create the “Birth to 3rd Grade (B-3) Partnership” – A city-wide collaborative focused on expanding access to and quality of early childhood programming across Cambridge, especially for lower-income families. • In 2017 the City Council committed to pursuing universal Pre-Kindergarten.

The B-3 Partnership began researching various UPK models. • In 2019 the B-3 Partnership presented UPK model options to City Council and the School Committee during a Joint Roundtable. Following the Partnership’s recommendation, City Council Committed to following a mixed delivery model. The B-3 Partnership began strategic planning to design this new mixed delivery system. • The B-3 Partnership became the Office of Early Childhood (OEC) in 2022.

Shortly thereafter, OEC began recruiting CPP program partners, and partnered with a software development firm to design the matching algorithm. • The CPP system launched in 2024: • Under the B-3 Partnership, a scholarship program was created that provided scholarships to 50 preschool-age children who met income-eligibility criteria. This was later expanded to serve 75 children.

The Partnership also included program quality support services for early childhood programs across Cambridge, including coaching, leadership mentoring, and professional development. • Winter 2024 – The first match was implemented. • September 2024 – The first group of CPP children started school. • October 2024 –The second round of matching was implemented for SY25-26. • Early 2025 – Families will be notified of their matches for SY25-26.

10 In addition to CPP, OEC works to enhance program quality across programs, providing quality supports to educators serving infants, toddlers, and preschoolers.

OEC Resources & Supports
