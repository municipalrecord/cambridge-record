• In 2025, the City Council asked the City Solicitor to review the Community Benefits Ordinance to draft an amendment that could allow community benefits funds to be used more broadly by nonprofit organizations, including for capital expenses (A.R. 25-40). •  The City Council also asked the City Solicitor to work with CBAC and other City departments to respond to this request. •  While coordinating with the Law department, the CBAC identified additional amendments that could potentially assist the Committee to more effectively distribute funds to nonprofits to meet resident needs. • In December 2025, the City Solicitor and CBAC responded to the Council with the proposed amendments.

Why We Are Here

Overview

Community Benefits & Advisory

Committee The Community Benefits Ordinance was adopted by council in 2015, and the

Community Benefits Advisory Committee was created to:

• Identify the pressing needs of Cambridge residents; • Evaluate applications by nonprofits to provide programs or services to meet needs; and • Make recommendations for grant awards supported by mitigation funds designated for community benefits Since 2017, the Community Benefits Advisory Committee has addressed the complex needs of Cambridge residents through: • Funding four partnerships of nonprofits (>$1M per partnership total) to respond to the top-tier needs of housing stability, financial security, and mental health • 2 rounds of Covid Emergency & Recovery grants of $10K each to >100 nonprofits each round (a total of $2M)

Community Benefits & Advisory Committee

Current Status

• Over $20 million has been pledged to the City for community benefits purposes, through zoning amendments and agreements • Approximately $8.6 million has been received; $7 million has been expended and $1.6 million remains. • The receipt of future contributions is dependent on development milestones being met, so timing of future funding is uncertain. It is possible that up to $3.2 million could be received over the next few years. • The Community Benefits committee is working toward an approach for recommending how to allocate remaining funds to meet residents’ needs

Current Status

Review Proposed Amendments

The City Solicitor opined that the community benefits funds are likely not subject to the Anti-Aid Amendment and therefore, can be used for capital and/or operating expenses, as long as they are advancing goals of the Community Benefits Ordinance.

This amendment could: • Provide flexibility for the CBAC to fund what could have the greatest impact • Allow flexibility for grantees to use funds where/how they are most needed • Help residents by building greater capacity for organizations to deliver impactful programs • Make the Community Benefits Stabilization Fund a more effective vehicle for funding nonprofits Proposed Amendment - Modify permissible use of funds to include capital and/or operating expenses There are many resources available that identify resident needs, including assessments done by Cambridge institutions on a regular basis (e.g. hospitals, Cambridge Public Health Department), and first tier needs are unlikely to change substantially over time.

This amendment could: • Lessen burden on residents to participate in duplicative assessment efforts • Support residents more consistently by potentially reducing time between funding cycles • Make funding priorities more predictable

Proposed Amendment – Modify needs assessment requirement

When the community benefits ordinance was established in 2015, it included 8 guiding principles. In 2017, the City Council adopted 9 additional guiding principles, for a total of 17. This amendment would: • Reflect the full set of Community Benefits guiding principles • Bring greater transparency to the values that drive the Committee’s actions and recommendations.
