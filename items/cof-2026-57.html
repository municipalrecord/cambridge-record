Research on Short-Term Cash Assistance

Short-term interventions can lead to positive, but modest, short-term effects • Short-term cash payments effectively solve some, but not all, economic challenges • No large-scale economic changes • Reduced food insecurity and mental distress • Limited evidence of effects on work • Numerous U.S. evaluations, varied study designs and program features 6

Rise Up Cambridge

Rise Up Cambridge built on Cambridge RISE and expanded eligibility to additional families Households with children at or under 21 years of age, earning up to 250 percent of the Federal Poverty Level First payments disbursed between June and August 2023. Final payments disbursed between December 2024 and February 2025. Households received $500 per month for 18 months ($9,000 total). 7

Research Questions

What are households’ perspectives on the program and cash payments? What outcomes did the Rise Up program generate for families? How did families with different characteristics and circumstances benefit from and experience the program? How is the program implemented and what is the policy context around this program in Cambridge?

8 ●Outcomes - Examine key indicators at study enrollment, one, and two years later ●Implementation - Examine Rise Up operations, participant experiences, contributions to observed outcomes ●Comparative Analysis - Examine differences based on household income and racial/ethnic identity 9 A longitudinal mixed-method design combining interviews, focus groups, surveys, and storytelling. Data collected at multiple points to examine changes in Rise Up families’ experiences and circumstances over time.

Research Design

Limitations Cannot make causal claims due to absence of a comparison group Quantitative survey data may not represent true early snapshot as responses drew on participant recollection Qualitative findings are not generalizable given relatively smaller number Strengths Using multiple data sources allows triangulation; verification across multiple data sources and methods Comparative approach shows outcomes and experiences of families with different characteristics Study period includes first 6 months after payments Centers racial equity and community engagement

EVALUATION FINDINGS

Participants were grateful for Rise Up, its features, and the support it provided. Contributed to reduced stress, a sense of empowerment, and more time with family. Families experienced meaningful financial relief during the program and incremental progress that may contribute to future economic mobility, but some hardships remained. Higher income families fare better. Some differences were observed across racial or ethnic groups. However, differences may reflect prior circumstances Extensive outreach and an online application made citywide reach possible. Rise Up did not count as income; participants maintained their other benefits. 12

Participant Perspectives

Participants reported less stress or anxiety 17 Having a little bit more wiggle room each month to make sure that the basics are covered. . . . At one point, I was working a side job, like, grading essays and things. That was kind of stressful, you know: staying up late grading these essays. I was able to stop doing that and actually get some sleep. So little things like that, it just sort of took the pressure off.

Participants reported spending more time with family 18 I was blessed to be able to join Rise Up Cambridge, which was able to get my son out of this area [neighborhood]. We were able to go to movies. We were able to go to the pool at least once a month. . . . It’s amazing when you can actually say, “You know what? This month, I don’t got to worry, and I can make plans to take my son to the movies and take him out to dinner.” It’s a good feeling.
