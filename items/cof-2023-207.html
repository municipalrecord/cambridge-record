After discussion with the Chair of the City Council's Ad-Hoc Committee on the City

Manager Evaluation Process, you have requested an opinion outlining Open Meeting Law and Public Records Law considerations concerning written evaluations used in the City Manager evaluation process. This opinion will be shared with the City Council to assist the Council with the evaluation process. The evaluation process will include the following documents: the City Manager's self- review, feedback from each City Councilor using the evaluation template, a consolidated review using the City Council feedback prepared by the Chair of the Ad-Hoc Committee, and feedback from the City Manager's direct reports and senior leadership team. As explained below: • the City Manager's self-review and the consolidated Council review will be public documents, while the feedback from each City Councilor, and the feedback from the City Manager's direct reports and senior leadership team, remain confidential personnel documents. Under the Public Records Law, all governmental records are presumed to be public records, unless the records or information contained therein are exempt from disclosure pursuant to one or more exemptions to the law under G.L. c. 4, §7(26). One of the exemptions addresses personnel records. That exemption provides that the following records are exempt: "personnel and medical files or information and any other materials or data relating to a specifically named Facsimile [phone removed] Telephone [phone removed] TTY/TTD [phone removed] individual, the disclosure of which may constitute an unwarranted invasion of personal privacy ..." G.L. c. 4, §7(26)(c). The Supreme Judicial Court has held that employee evaluations may be withheld pursuant to exemption (c). Wakefield Teachers Ass'n v. Sch. Comm., 431 Mass. 792, 798 (2000). Therefore, the starting point is that all evaluations used in the City Manager evaluation process may be withheld from disclosure as confidential personnel documents. However, since the evaluation of the City Manager is being performed by the City Council, the Public Records Law intersects with the Open Meeting Law.

The Open Meeting Law states that:

The minutes of any open session, the notes, recordings or other materials used in the preparation of such minutes and all documents and exhibits used at the session, shall be public records in their entirety and not exempt from disclosure pursuant to any of the exemptions under clause Twenty-sixth of section 7 of chapter 4.

Notwithstanding this paragraph, the following materials shall be exempt from disclosure to the public as personnel information: (1) materials used in a performance evaluation of an individual bearing on his professional competence, provided they were not created by the members of the body for the purposes of the evaluation;... G.L. c.30A, §22(e). (Emphasis added).

This section provides that, as a general rule, a document used at a public meeting becomes a public record, even if it would otherwise be exempt from the Public Records Law. However, this section goes on to distinguish personnel evaluations differently than the general rule. This section provides that if a performance evaluation that is not prepared by the public body, here the City Council, is used at a public meeting, it remains a confidential personnel record that may be withheld under the Public Record Law.

Therefore, while the feedback from the City Manager's direct reports and senior leadership team may be provided to the City Council and used at a meeting of the City Council, it remains a confidential personnel record exempt from disclosure.

Additionally, if the individual feedback from each City Councilor is not discussed at a meeting of the City Council, and instead the City Council only discusses the consolidated review prepared by the Chair of the Ad-Hoc Committee, the individual feedback documents are not being used at the meeting and therefore can remain confidential personnel records. Lastly, the City Manager has provided his self-review to the Council to be publicly posted on the Council's agenda. Very truly yours, Megan B. Bayer meeaklay

Acting City Solicitor
