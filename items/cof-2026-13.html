Executive Summary

Last week, we shared an update on how we are planning for the FY27 operating budget amid economic headwinds and federal uncertainty Our capital budget differs from our operating budget in its timing, purpose, and funding sources, but capital decisions affect our operating budget through the payment of debt service Over the past ten years, we have invested in many sewer, water, schools, open space, streets, and municipal buildings capital projects across the City At the same time, our tax-supported debt service has grown faster than the operating budget in all but two of the past ten years Meeting our target of 5% tax-supported debt service growth in the long-term will require careful prioritization and understanding of new and existing projects We are continuing to work with departments to identify opportunities to reduce our five-year bond schedule by project type (e.g., open space, municipal building, streets, etc.) Beyond FY29, we have set aside minimum required bonding amounts for each project type based on minimum program needs, though there are large projects identified by departments as future needs that have yet to be included within the bond schedule Last week, we shared an update on the operating budget, which differs from our capital budget in key ways Operating Capital Timing ▪Funds must be expended within fiscal year ▪Funds carry over until project is completed Purpose ▪Funds day to day expenses (e.g., community programs, City administration, resident services) ▪Funds the purchase and construction of buildings, land, parks, streets, sewers, and equipment Examples ▪Salaries and benefits ▪Supplies (e.g., road salt) ▪Training ▪Exterior improvements to City Hall ▪Reconstruction of River Street ▪Purchase of fire trucks, garbage trucks Funding Sources ▪Primarily property taxes, as well as other sources like licenses & permits ▪Primarily bond proceeds, as well as other sources like water and sewer service charges and free cash

Spending The City’s capital budget is a plan for future capital program spending. Loan orders for projects in the capital budget require City Council approval in the form of a loan authorization. A bond schedule is organized based on the cash flow needs of each individual project, ensuring the City only issues new debt when it is needed. For the life of the bond, the City is obligated to pay principal and interest payments, or debt service.

Once bonds are sold, 95% of the bond issuance should be spent within 2 years. Bond issuances create debt, which can be structured on 5-, 10-, or 20-year terms, in exchange for the funds for the project. Bonding for capital projects is a multi-step process that involves authorizing a loan, issuing the bond, paying down debt, and spending
