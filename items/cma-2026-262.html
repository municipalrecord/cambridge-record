Rita Rzezuski

Cambridge has long recognized the importance of early childcare and childhood education, establishing policies and programs that support children and families during their formative years. Decades of research confirm that high-quality early childhood experiences are crucial for lifelong learning, school success, and future economic stability. Recognizing these benefits, the City implemented the Cambridge Preschool Program (CPP), offering universal, free preschool to all four-year-olds and many three-year-olds, with additional support for families with lower incomes.

Despite progress, challenges remain for families in accessing affordable, high-quality care, as reflected in community feedback. Most acutely, families have indicated the need for more affordable, high-quality childcare options for infants and toddlers, who are not yet eligible for CPP. In response, the City Council prioritized expanding childcare as a top goal in 2026, leading to the creation of this joint Task Force. The Task Force is charged with examining the current childcare landscape, exploring strategies to improve access, and developing policy proposals aligned with the City’s planning priorities.

Members were selected to represent a broad spectrum of expertise and perspectives. The Task Force includes City Council, City staff, and subject-matter experts in early childhood education, childcare finance, and workforce development. The recommended appointees were chosen for their experience and commitment to advancing equitable, accessible, and high-quality early childhood services in Cambridge. Additional members may be added to further enhance the group’s diversity and expertise, and engagement methods will be built in to ensure that work is grounded in existing and new feedback from families and providers.

The Task Force is intended to advance this important work through a coordinated, structured, and community-informed process that will help Cambridge continue its leadership in building an equitable, accessible, and high-quality early childhood ecosystem.

In addition to the recommended appointees, the Task Force will include:

Sumbul Siddiqui, Mayor

Marc C. McGovern, City Councillor Patricia M. Nolan, City Councillor

Additional members may be added as needed to further strengthen the group’s diversity and expertise.

Member Bios

Joseph Corazzini

Joseph Corazzini (he/his) serves as the Assistant City Manager for the Department of Human Services in Cambridge. Prior to this role he served as the Vice President of Government and Community Affairs at Clark University. There he led the university's government relations efforts serving as a vital liaison between the institution and the Main South community.

Joseph brings extensive experience having held senior leadership positions in multiple sectors focused on education, community development, and advancing equity. Prior to joining Clark University, he served as the Assistant Superintendent of Equity, Diversity, and Community Development for Framingham Public Schools. There he spearheaded a range of equity-driven initiatives designed to close institutional gaps for under-resourced students.

His efforts led to the creation and expansion of several impactful programs, including district-run Out of School Time programming, early college pathways, universal pre-K, diverse teacher recruitment pipelines, a holistic wraparound services model, a districtwide DEI professional development strategy, and a comprehensive family welcome center. Joseph holds a Bachelor of Arts in History and a Master of Science in Nonprofit Management from Worcester State University.

He is also a proud graduate of the AASA/Howard University Urban Superintendents Academy.

Katy Donovan

Katy Donovan has worked in early childhood in Cambridge for over 40 years in just about every role an early childhood program has to offer. Most of her work was at Peabody Terrace Children's Center. When the Harvard affiliated programs decided to merge in 2017, the other directors within that system asked her to take the helm. As an independent non-profit on Harvard's campus, Campus Child Care employs over 220 early childhood professionals and cares for about 425 children daily in 7 locations.

Katy truly loves the challenges that come to early childhood leadership every day; whether it be figuring out the puzzle of staffing, counseling parents and teachers or just getting a chance to support a child through a difficult moment. Katy believes that this is very special work and children deserve our full attention.

In the executive director's role the daily tasks can look different, but remain grounded in the principles that what is best for children and their caregivers is the best choice for the organization and its families, even if it is sometimes hard.

Beyond her direct work on campus, she is proud to have served with multiple groups that support both the City and the University including the Early Education Task Force (pre-CPP) examining the delivery of city-wide access to and affordability of high quality child care for all residents and the Steering Committee of the Early Years Project bringing city-wide support to classrooms and parents around challenging behaviors.

Katy served on the Board of the Boston Area Reggio Inspired Network, a group whose work brings high quality training to the early childhood community. When not at work she enjoys gardening, and especially spending time with family, including 3 grown children and a new grandchild.

Michelle Godfrey

Michelle Godfrey is the Director of the Community Engagement and Family Support Division at the City of Cambridge’s Department of Human Service Programs. Michelle takes pride in working in and for the city she grew up in and has spent more than three decades advocating for families and designing programs that honor their strengths, address challenges, and amplify their voices.

As a mother and now a Mimi, Michelle understands the joys and complexities of parenting. Her personal experiences, combined with her extensive professional background, shape her commitment to evidence-based family support practices that meet the diverse needs of Cambridge families.

Michelle joined the Center for Families in 2008, working with a small and creative team that collaborated closely with the community to understand barriers and build programming rooted in trust, cultural humility, and strong relationships. This foundational work helped evolve the Center for Families into today’s Community Engagement and Family Support Division.
