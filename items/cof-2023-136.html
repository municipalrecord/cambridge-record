Between January and March 2023, the Board’s work included the following:

The Board reviewed the FY 2024 Cambridge Police Department (CPD) budget before

submission to the City Manager as specified in Cambridge Municipal Code Chapter 2.74.040(B). CPD Commissioner Elow attended the March 29, 2023 Board meeting to discuss key topics with the Board, with a focus on the CPD’s efforts in the wake of the fatal police shooting of Arif Sayed Faisal in January. Discussion topics included the implementing body-worn cameras (BWC), having an external review of de-escalation training and work, and how the Board can provide input into those efforts.

The Board has received five draft CPD policies for review, including the draft policy governing the use of body-worn cameras (BWCs), as part of the Board’s goal of conducting a review of a minimum of two CPD policies.

Minutes from the Meeting of January 25, 2023

Board Members present: Gina LaRoche, Lucy Murray-Brown, Beverly Sealey

City Staff present: Executive Director Brian Corr; CPD Director of Professional Standards,

Training and Certification James Mulcahy; CPD Sergeant Michael LeVecque

Acting Chair LaRoche called the meeting to order at 6:08 p.m. Acting Chair LaRoche briefly stated a summary of the Board’s role and purpose:

The Police Review & Advisory Board provides a prompt, impartial and fair investigation of complaints brought by individuals against members of the Cambridge Police Department.

The Police Review & Advisory Board was established by Cambridge City Ordinance in 1984 to: Provide for citizen participation in reviewing Police Department policies, practices, and procedures; Provide a prompt, impartial and fair investigation of complaints brought by individuals against members of the Cambridge Police Department; and Develop programs and strategies to promote positive police/community relations and to provide opportunities for expanded discussions, improved understanding, and innovative ways of resolving differences.

Sue Reinert, a reporter for Cambridge Day, stated that she was recording the meeting.

The Board voted unanimously by roll call vote to approve the minutes of December 7, 2022, on a motion from Board Member Murray-Brown, seconded by Board Member Sealey.

Exec. Dir. Corr provided an update on the process for filling vacancies of the Board: Update on Board vacancy: The City Manager’s Office received almost 30 applications for the two vacancies on the Police Review & Advisory Board (the “Board”), posted on November 9 and closing on December 12. Interviews took place during mid-December. Under the city charter, the City Council votes on whether to approve the City Manager's proposed appointments to City boards and commissions.

Between the number of boards and commissions with pending appointments that need to go to the City Council and the fact that there are only three City Council meetings scheduled between December 19, 2022 and February 6, 2023 and tragically, the recent officer-involved fatal shooting of Sayed Faisal, the City Manager has not yet made recommendations to the Council for their approval. Exec. Dir. Corr stated that he is hopeful that appointments will be made by the next meeting of the Board. Exec. Dir.

Corr explained that Chair Fallon would be unable to attend the next scheduled meeting on Wednesday, February 22 and asked if Board members would be able to attend if the date were shifted to Wednesday, March 1, and all board members present stated that

they would be able to attend, with the proviso that it begins at 6:15 p.m. The subsequent meeting will remain scheduled for Wednesday, March 29.

Dir. Mulcahy noted that the fifth class of the joint Cambridge/Northeastern University Police Academy class started this month with a robust group of student officers from CPD and other agencies, including 10 CPD student officers. This academy class is in its third week. Dir. Mulcahy will be teaching classes on Constitutional law, criminal law, and criminal investigations. Dir.

Mulcahy provided an update on in-service training for all currently serving CPD officers: He reported that the class-based in-service training for 2023 has begun for all currently serving CPD officers, and each January the Unit begins training using the Massachusetts Municipal Police Training Committee (MPTC) curriculum of four courses. In the fall they conduct training on use of force and firearms certification.

In addition, each year there is a theme across the MPTC training curriculum, and this year the theme is trauma-informed policing, which CPD has been training on for many years. and they will cycle through until all officers have met their annual in-service training requirement. In response to a question from Board Member Sealey about the possibility of board members attending in-service training sessions, Dir.

Mulcahy explained that they are conducting the in-service training through MPTC’s online training portal, and that there are parts of that curriculum which they could work to make available to board members and that CPD would need to set up MPTC accounts for them. The trainings will be available on- demand over the upcoming weeks and months until all CPD officers have completed this training cycle.

He also noted that the CPD has a scenario-based training simulator which would be valuable for board members to experience. In response to a question from Board Member LaRoche the process of accreditation, Dir. Mulcahy reported that the Unit has finished rewriting all CPD policies and procedures, but there has been a delay with the command staff review, and that has delayed providing them to the officers. Dir.

Mulcahy hopes to have everything done and so that CPD will be accredited by the Commission on Accreditation for Law Enforcement Agencies (CALEA) by early 2024
